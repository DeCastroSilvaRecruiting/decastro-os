# Treasury Liquidity Tiers

> **Bottom line:** Cash has three jobs — paying this month's bills, surviving a bad year, and seizing opportunities. Give each job its own tier, size the first two in months of burn, and only deploy what sits in the third.

## The three tiers
| Tier | Purpose | Size | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operating** | Bills, payroll, everyday spend | 1–3 months of burn | Current account |
| **Reserve** | Job loss, vacancy, venture overrun, emergencies | 6–12+ months of burn | Instant-access savings / money-market, not invested in risk assets |
| **Strategic** | Dry powder for deals and ventures | Everything above | Higher-yield deposits or short-term instruments matched to when you might deploy |

The idea mirrors how banks are required to hold enough high-quality liquid assets to survive a 30-day stress (Basel LCR): survive first, earn second.

## Runway
**Runway = cash ÷ monthly burn.** For a founder with variable income, aim for the reserve to cover at least 12 months of personal burn before committing serious capital to a venture.

## Rules
1. Refill the reserve before deploying strategic capital.
2. Each venture keeps its own operating and reserve tiers — never borrow from personal reserve without writing it down as a loan.
3. Match maturities: money you may need in 3 months should not be in a 1-year deposit.
4. Diversify banks for large balances; check the deposit protection that applies to each.
5. Hold currencies you will spend in; AED is pegged to USD, other currencies carry FX risk.

## Key takeaways
- Size reserves in months of burn, not round numbers.
- Strategic cash is the only cash you deploy.
- Runway is a number to know every month.
