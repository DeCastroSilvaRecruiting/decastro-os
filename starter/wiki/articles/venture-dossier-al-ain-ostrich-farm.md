# Venture Dossier: Al Ain Ostrich Farm

> **Bottom line:** Before any business plan, three gates must open. **Permission:** ADAFSA's 2024 farm-activity framework lists sheep, camels, cows, poultry and aquaculture — not ostriches — and allows animal production only where water scarcity or salinity (>10,000 ppm) prevents crops; the Abu Dhabi Farm Licence is for UAE nationals who own farms. **Supply:** the UAE banned live birds from South Africa in 2019 over avian flu (current status unverified). **Competition:** **Ostrich Oasis** in Al Ain already runs ~2,000 birds and 75 t of meat a year with an abattoir and tannery. The most realistic path may be partnering with an Emirati farm owner — or with Ostrich Oasis itself — and leading on the visitor experience rather than the breeding.

## Context
Decision informed: whether to commit capital to a greenfield ostrich farm and visitor destination in Al Ain (water hole, craft store, butchery, restaurant, South African expertise), or reshape the concept. See [[Venture Validation Framework]].

## Production biology (benchmarks)
| Metric | Benchmark | Source |

| Eggs per hen per season | 30–40 typical; 35–55 average in US extension data; up to 80–100 at best | Harper Adams 1998; Texas A&M |
| Hatchability | ~50% of set eggs (SA, 2006); ~70% under good management | Cloete 2006; Harper Adams |
| Chick mortality | 21–26% to 30 days; **50–62% to 1 year** (Oudtshoorn research flock) | SASAS 2016 [T1] |
| Commercial survival | "topping 85%" to ~4 months (2026) | Farmer's Weekly [T2] |

| Breeders' intake | males ~3.1, females ~2.6 kg/day | SASAS 2016 |

## South African industry (Oudtshoorn / Klein Karoo)
- SA holds ~75% of the global market; >150 t of feathers exported in 2024/25 (AFP, Sep 2026).
- Slaughter fell from ~300,000 birds (2002) to ~150,000 (2025) amid a 7+ year drought (Farmer's Weekly, Jul 2026).
- Exports of meat, feathers and leather: R578.8m (2021, Western Cape Govt).
- Research-farm breeding birds at the 2023 Oudtshoorn auction averaged **R9,996 (males) and R21,630 (females)**.
- EU fresh-meat access has been interrupted by avian influenza (ban 2011, lifted 2015); current status unverified.
- **Reputational risk:** a Sep 2026 welfare exposé alleges live plucking in the SA feather trade, naming major fashion houses.

## UAE permissions (as of Nov 2026)
- **Farm activities:** ADAFSA Board Resolution 3/2023 (published Mar 2024) — animal activities only where water scarcity or salinity >10,000 ppm prevents crops; species listed: sheep, camels, cows, poultry, aquaculture, each needing ADAFSA approval; ≥25% of farm planted for plant activities; applications via TAMM. **Ostriches not named.**
- **Farm Licence:** for **UAE nationals who own private farms**; Decision 5/2025 expanded permitted activities to 145, including recreational and heritage activities; Al Ain holds 896 active agricultural licences (63% of the emirate, Q3 2025).
- **Imports (MOCCAE fees, Cabinet Resolution 152/2025):** import permit for live birds/hatching eggs AED 100; veterinary health certificate AED 400; hatching-egg release AED 500; birds AED 1 each (min 200); chicks AED 0.60.
- **South African birds:** banned July 2019 "until further notice" (Gulf News) — confirm current status with MOCCAE.
- **Slaughter and food service:** mobile slaughter units need a commercial licence + ADAFSA NOC (2022); on-farm abattoir and restaurant licensing rules not found this run.

## Competition and demand
- **Ostrich Oasis Farm, Nahel (Al Ain):** owner Fahd Saeed Al Ketbi; started from 15 imported birds; ~2,000 birds; ~75 t meat/yr (Gulf News, Jan 2026); founded 2012 on 68,000 m²; halal abattoir, tannery, mini-mart, camping; South African managing director; plans for café, restaurant, education centre (Khaleej Times, 2023).
- **Tourism:** Abu Dhabi had 26.6m visitors in 2025; **Al Ain 473,100 hotel guests in 2025 (+9%)**; target ~520,000 leisure guests by 2030. Al Ain Zoo: 10m visitors cumulatively since 2010 (~0.75m/yr average, derived).

## Worked example — herd arithmetic (illustrative; assumptions labelled)
Start with **30 breeding pairs** (assumption).
- Eggs: 30 hens × 40 = 1,200
- Chicks hatched at 50% = 600
- Survivors to slaughter at 50% (SA research mortality) = **300 birds**; at 85% survival (commercial best) ≈ 510
- Meat at 32 kg/bird: **9.6 t** (300 birds) to 16.3 t (510)
- Feed for slaughter birds: ~2 kg/day × ~320 days ≈ 640 kg each → **192 t/yr** for 300 birds, plus breeders ~0.3 t/day × 365 ≈ 104 t
- Breeding stock at 2023 SA auction averages: 30 × (R9,996 + R21,630) ≈ R949k ≈ **AED 190k** at an assumed ~R4.9/AED — before import permits, shipping and quarantine, and *if* SA imports are allowed.
Judgement: at this scale meat output is ~1/8 of Ostrich Oasis; the farm cannot win on meat volume. Visitor revenue (entry, restaurant, craft store) must carry the economics — breeding is the attraction, not the profit centre.

## Capital (estimates only)
No published UAE cost model found. The only full model (UK, 1998, 100 hens, ~79 ha) totalled ~£323k with fencing the largest item — dated and climatically irrelevant. In Al Ain, shade, cooling, water (possibly desalinated), an enclosed hatchery and visitor infrastructure will dominate. **Get 3 contractor quotes before any figure enters the plan.**

## Riskiest assumptions (judgement) — test in this order
1. **Ostriches are permitted** on an Al Ain farm, and a non-national can operate there (lease or partnership). *Test:* written ADAFSA/ADRA enquiry via TAMM.
2. **Stock can be sourced** (SA ban status; local purchase from Ostrich Oasis; eggs vs birds). *Test:* MOCCAE enquiry.
3. **Differentiation vs Ostrich Oasis** — or partnership with it. *Test:* visit; explore a joint visitor experience.
4. **Visitor demand** for a paid farm attraction given ~0.47m Al Ain hotel guests/yr. *Test:* survey and comparables (date farms, camel farms, Al Ain Zoo pricing).
5. **Biology in Al Ain heat:** hatchability and chick survival. *Test:* ask Ostrich Oasis for its real figures.
6. **Feed cost** — 70–80% of costs, largely imported.

## Key takeaways
- Permission and stock supply are the gating risks; resolve them before spending.
- A state-backed incumbent already operates the same model in Al Ain.
- Biology is slow: first income ~30 months after hatching; first-year mortality can reach 50–60%.
- A small farm can't compete on meat; the visitor offer must carry it.
- Feather/welfare scrutiny is rising — design for high welfare and say so.

## What this means for the empire
- Keep the venture at **Idea/Validate**; spend nothing beyond enquiries and a site visit until gates 1–2 are answered in writing.
- Reframe the concept as an **agri-tourism destination with ostriches**, ideally on an Emirati partner's licensed farm or in partnership with Ostrich Oasis, rather than a production farm.
- Expect losses for 3+ years — don't elect Small Business Relief in loss years so losses carry forward ([[UAE Corporate Tax Essentials]]).
- Abu Dhabi farm tenure differs from Dubai freehold; structure separately ([[UAE Holding Structures]]).

## Open questions
- ADAFSA position on ratites and non-national farm leases.
- MOCCAE current status on South African birds and hatching eggs.
- Abu Dhabi licensing for on-farm abattoir, butchery and restaurant.
- Current SA producer prices (R/kg meat, per skin) and feather yield per bird.
- Visitor pricing and footfall at Al Ain farm attractions.
