# Venture Dossier: Al Ain Ostrich Farm

> **Bottom line:** Before any business plan, three gates must open. **Permission:** ADAFSA's 2024 farm-activity framework lists sheep, camels, cows, poultry and aquaculture — not ostriches — and allows animal production only where water scarcity or salinity (>10,000 ppm) prevents crops. The Abu Dhabi Farm Licence is for UAE nationals who own farms, but the 2025 amendment lets **"farm owners or licensed establishments"** carry out the 145 approved farm activities, which opens a route for a licensed operating company on an Emirati partner's farm (to be confirmed with ADAFSA). **Supply:** the UAE banned live birds from South Africa in July 2019 over avian flu "until further notice"; a Nov 2026 search found no published lifting (the previous ban was lifted by Ministerial Decree 488 of 2009, so lifts are published when they happen). **Competition:** **Ostrich Oasis** in Al Ain already runs ~2,000 birds and 75 t of meat a year with an abattoir and tannery. The most realistic path may be partnering with an Emirati farm owner — or with Ostrich Oasis itself — and leading on the visitor experience rather than the breeding. The timing helps: in Oct 2026 ADAFSA and Ma'an launched **Nama**, a pilot to turn underused Al Ain farms into agri-tourism destinations, so farm owners are now being encouraged to do exactly this. That experience earns money only in the cool season: comparable UAE farm attractions close for 3–6 summer months.

## Context
Decision informed: whether to commit capital to a greenfield ostrich farm and visitor destination in Al Ain (water hole, craft store, butchery, restaurant, South African expertise), or reshape the concept. See [[Venture Validation Framework]].

## South African industry (Oudtshoorn / Klein Karoo)
- SA holds ~75% of the global market; >150 t of feathers exported in 2024/25 (AFP, Sep 2026).
- Slaughter fell from ~300,000 birds (2002) to ~150,000 (2025) amid a 7+ year drought (Farmer's Weekly, Jul 2026).
- Slaughter was forecast at ~140,000 birds in 2023/24 rising ~7% to ~150,000 in 2024/25; feed costs had fallen after the drought broke (more lucerne), but some breeders reported **up to 60% lower egg output and 50% fewer chicks** over two mating seasons; meat markets "stagnant for several years"; leather demand good (France and Italy ≈ 50% of the leather market, plus the US); feather demand down in China and the EU (Farmer's Weekly, Jan 2025 [T2]).
- Exports of meat, feathers and leather: R578.8m (2021, Western Cape Govt).
- Research-farm breeding birds at the 2023 Oudtshoorn auction averaged **R9,996 (males) and R21,630 (females)**.
- EU fresh-meat access has been interrupted by avian influenza (ban 2011, lifted 2015); current status unverified.
- **Reputational risk:** a Sep 2026 welfare exposé alleges live plucking in the SA feather trade, naming major fashion houses.

## UAE permissions (as of Nov 2026)
- **Farm activities:** ADAFSA Board Resolution 3/2023 (published Mar 2024) — animal activities only where water scarcity or salinity >10,000 ppm prevents crops; species listed: sheep, camels, cows, poultry, aquaculture, each needing ADAFSA approval; ≥25% of farm planted for plant activities; applications via TAMM. **Ostriches not named.**
- **Farm Licence:** for **UAE nationals who own private farms**; Al Ain holds 896 active agricultural licences (63% of the emirate, Q3 2025).
- **Decision 5 of 2025** (ADAFSA Board, published 12 Sep 2025) amends Decision 3/2023 and adds 74 activities, taking the total to **145**: 41 plant production, 9 supporting plant production, 8 food support services, 12 industrial support, 2 general support services and **2 recreational**. Key wording: *"Farm owners or licensed establishments in the Emirate of Abu Dhabi may practise economic activities on farms"*, provided the farm is used for its designated purpose and built-up area stays within the limit set by the competent authority. A committee under the Abu Dhabi Executive Office including ADDED, DMT and DCT handles licensing. The published summaries **do not list the individual activities**, so whether animal attractions, on-farm restaurants, butcheries or ratites are among them is **unverified** — get the full activity list via TAMM [T1, Abu Dhabi Media Office].
- **Farm stays:** since Aug 2023 DCT Abu Dhabi issues **holiday-home licences for farmhouses** (one licence per unit) [T1]; by Sep 2025, 29 farms had holiday-home permits under the 2023 decision (Abu Dhabi Media Office). Overnight farm stays are therefore an already-licensable revenue line.
- **Nama programme (launched Oct 2026):** the Authority of Social Contribution (**Ma'an**) and **ADAFSA** are piloting the conversion of selected underused **Al Ain** farms into destinations that "blend agriculture, tourism and business", with farm assessments, technical and operational support, and agri-tourism awareness campaigns; Ma'an handles governance, finance and partner coordination. Number of farms, names, eligibility and application route had **not been announced** (Curly Tales, 21 Oct 2026 [T3]; the WAM original could not be read this run). Judgement: Nama-selected farm owners are the natural partners for this venture — they will want an operator with a concept.
- **Imports (MOCCAE fees, Cabinet Resolution 152/2025):** import permit for live birds/hatching eggs AED 100; veterinary health certificate AED 400; hatching-egg release AED 500; birds AED 1 each (min 200); chicks AED 0.60.
- **South African birds — history:** the UAE lifted an earlier ban on "all kinds of living birds and their products" from South Africa by **Ministerial Decree 488 of 2009** (12 Oct 2009), excluding areas with bird-flu reports and subject to veterinary quarantine law [T1 via UNEP LEAP]. It **re-banned live birds from South Africa in July 2019** (with ruminants from Madagascar) as a precaution "until further notice" (Gulf News, 23 Jul 2019). Neither text names ostriches; ostriches are birds, so assume they are covered.
- **Status as of Nov 2026:** no decree or ministry notice lifting the 2019 ban was found in searches this run (MOCCAE site, UNEP LEAP, Gulf News, Khaleej Times). South Africa's own DALRRD export-conditions notice for the UAE ban could not be opened (site blocks automated access). **Unverified — confirm in writing with MOCCAE**, and ask separately about hatching eggs, which carry their own MOCCAE fee line and may be treated differently.
- **Other sources of stock:** the first UAE ostrich farm (Al Ain, 1997) imported its flock from **Namibia** (Gulf News, 2005). Namibia, local purchase from existing UAE flocks, or hatching eggs are the alternatives to test with MOCCAE.
- **Farm tenancy (Izab plots, DMT, Oct 2026):** Abu Dhabi's Department of Municipalities and Transport requires tenants to start the approved activity within the set period, keep a **minimum number of livestock for the plot size** (also checked at renewal), stay within lease boundaries and use designated waste sites. Breaches can lead to fines and **repossession without advance notice**; on exit the plot must be restored. The report does not say whether expatriates may hold these leases or sublet — ask DMT (Gulf News, 24 Oct 2026 [T2]).
- **Animal tagging:** ADAFSA requires livestock to be tagged and registered; selling, buying or moving untagged animals is prohibited (Gulf News, Sep 2025). Reports name camels and cattle; whether ratites are covered is not stated.
- **Slaughter and food service:** mobile slaughter units need a commercial licence + ADAFSA NOC (2022); on-farm abattoir and restaurant licensing rules not found this run.

## Competition and demand
- **History is a base rate:** by 2005 five farms in Al Sa'ad (Al Ain) bred ostriches, the largest with ~100 birds; the market was "not big yet" and sales were mainly birds, meat and eggs to farm visitors; ostrich races were tried in 2002 and not repeated (Gulf News, Feb 2005). Twenty years later one scaled operator (Ostrich Oasis) is visible. Judgement: small Al Ain ostrich farms have been tried before and mostly did not scale — the plan must say why this one is different.
- **Ostrich Oasis Farm, Nahel (Al Ain):** owner Fahd Saeed Al Ketbi; started from 15 imported birds; ~2,000 birds; ~75 t meat/yr (Gulf News, Jan 2026); founded 2012 on 68,000 m²; halal abattoir, tannery, mini-mart, camping; South African managing director; plans for café, restaurant, education centre (Khaleej Times, 2023).
- **Tourism:** Abu Dhabi had 26.6m visitors in 2025; **Al Ain 473,100 hotel guests in 2025 (+9%)**; H1 2025 alone 228,000 (+12%), with Al Ain Oasis visits +40% (Abu Dhabi Media Office). Target ~520,000 leisure guests by 2030. Al Ain Zoo: 10m visitors cumulatively since 2010 (~0.75m/yr average, derived). Al Ain is the **Arab Capital of Tourism 2026**, with a year-long DCT programme. The relaunched Al Ain Oasis night experience (Sura) drew ~100,000 visitors in two months in 2026 (Gulf News, Sep 2026) — evidence that new paid-for experiences in Al Ain can draw crowds.

## Worked example — herd arithmetic (illustrative; assumptions labelled)
Start with **30 breeding pairs** (assumption).
- Eggs: 30 hens × 40 = 1,200
- Chicks hatched at 50% = 600
- Survivors to slaughter at 50% (SA research mortality) = **300 birds**; at 85% survival (commercial best) ≈ 510
- Meat at 32 kg/bird: **9.6 t** (300 birds) to 16.3 t (510)
- Feed for slaughter birds: ~2 kg/day × ~320 days ≈ 640 kg each → **192 t/yr** for 300 birds, plus breeders ~0.3 t/day × 365 ≈ 104 t
- Breeding stock at 2023 SA auction averages: 30 × (R9,996 + R21,630) ≈ R949k ≈ **AED 190k** at an assumed ~R4.9/AED — before import permits, shipping and quarantine, and *if* SA imports are allowed.
Judgement: at this scale meat output is ~1/8 of Ostrich Oasis; the farm cannot win on meat volume. Visitor revenue (entry, restaurant, craft store) must carry the economics — breeding is the attraction, not the profit centre.

## Worked example — can visitors pay for the birds? (illustrative; assumptions labelled)
- **Feed bill:** from the herd example, ~296 t/yr (192 t slaughter birds + 104 t breeders). A ratite-specific feed price in the UAE was **not found**. The best public benchmark: ADAFSA's Fodder Market sold ~59,000 t for AED 97.2m in its first two weeks (Jul 2023, Abu Dhabi Media Office [T1]) — an implied **average of ~AED 1,650/t (AED 1.65/kg)** across all grass and compound feed. Using that: 296 t × AED 1.65 ≈ **AED 488,000/yr**. Treat it as a *floor*: it is a 2023 average across grass and compound feed, and a formulated ratite ration is likely to cost more (judgement). Feed is 75–80% of rearing cost in South Africa (Farmer's Weekly, Jul 2026), so total bird costs ≈ **AED 610–650k/yr**.
- **Visitor contribution:** *assume* AED 40 entry + AED 60 spend on food and retail at 30% margin = **AED 58 per visitor**.
- **Break-even on feed alone:** 488,000 ÷ 58 ≈ **8,400 visitors/yr** ≈ 40/day over a 210-day season. To cover all bird costs (~AED 630k): ≈ 10,900 visitors ≈ **52/day**.
- **Display-flock alternative (illustrative):** 20 adult birds at ~2.6–3.1 kg/day ≈ 21 t/yr ≈ **AED 35,000/yr** of feed at the same price — about 600 visitors' contribution. This is why the attraction should not carry a breeding operation.
- **Before** staff, water, cooling, depreciation of capex and licences. At ~0.47m Al Ain hotel guests a year, 10,000 visitors is ~2% — plausible only with Al Ain residents and Dubai/Abu Dhabi day-trippers.
Judgement: the birds are cheap relative to the visitor upside, but a breeding flock of 60 adults is far more than an attraction needs. A **small display flock bought locally** (e.g. from Ostrich Oasis) removes the import gate and most of the feed bill.

## How Oudtshoorn show farms make money (comparables)
Oudtshoorn's long-running ostrich show farms sell a **guided tour plus an ostrich meal**, not entry alone. **Highgate** runs a ~1h20 tour (incubators, anatomy, hand-feeding, breeding camp, feather-product factory) bundled with a 3-course lunch or dinner of ostrich egg and meat (~2h20 in total, minimum 10 people), and sells eggs and feather products (Highgate site [T3]). **Safari Ostrich** runs a tractor safari through breeder camps and chick-rearing areas, an information centre where visitors handle leather, feathers and carved eggs, and a restaurant (Wesgro [T2]). Neither page publishes prices or visitor numbers.
Judgement: the proven format is **tour + meal + shop**, sold to groups. For Al Ain that suggests pricing a bundled ticket (tour + ostrich lunch) rather than a low gate fee, and courting school groups, corporate days (a link to [[Venture Dossier: Wednesday Circuit]]) and Dubai/Abu Dhabi day-trip operators.

## What South Africa's experience adds (2026)
- Producers aim to be **within 150 km of a processor and 200 km of cheap feed** (Dr Adriaan Olivier, Farmer's Weekly, Jul 2026). In Al Ain the only known ostrich abattoir is Ostrich Oasis, and feed is largely imported — another argument for partnering rather than competing.
- Hatcheries sell 200–400 day-old chicks at a time; small producers raise 50–100 chicks as a side enterprise; commercial survival now tops 85% to ~4 months (Farmer's Weekly, Jul 2026).
- Ostrich eggs sell for R60–90 in formal outlets and R300–600 informally (George Herald, Feb 2026 [T3]) — eggs are a cheap, visual retail product for the craft store.

## Capital (estimates only)
No published UAE cost model found. The only full model (UK, 1998, 100 hens, ~79 ha) totalled ~£323k with fencing the largest item — dated and climatically irrelevant. In Al Ain, shade, cooling, water (possibly desalinated), an enclosed hatchery and visitor infrastructure will dominate. **Get 3 contractor quotes before any figure enters the plan.**

## Riskiest assumptions (judgement) — test in this order
1. **Ostriches are permitted** on an Al Ain farm, and a non-national can operate there through a licensed establishment (Decision 5/2025 wording) on a partner's farm. *Test:* written ADAFSA/ADRA enquiry via TAMM asking for the full 145-activity list and whether ratites, an animal attraction, a restaurant and a butchery are covered.
2. **Stock can be sourced** (SA ban status; local purchase from Ostrich Oasis; eggs vs birds). *Test:* MOCCAE enquiry.
3. **Differentiation vs Ostrich Oasis** — or partnership with it. *Test:* visit; explore a joint visitor experience.
4. **Visitor demand** for a paid farm attraction given ~0.47m Al Ain hotel guests/yr. *Test:* survey and comparables (date farms, camel farms, Al Ain Zoo pricing).
5. **Biology in Al Ain heat:** hatchability and chick survival. *Test:* ask Ostrich Oasis for its real figures.
6. **Feed cost** — 70–80% of costs, largely imported. *Test:* quotes from two UAE feed mills for ratite feed.
7. **Seasonality:** a 5–7 month trading year. *Test:* model monthly cash flow with zero visitor revenue June–September.

## Key takeaways
- Permission and stock supply are the gating risks; resolve them before spending.
- A state-backed incumbent already operates the same model in Al Ain.
- Biology is slow: first income ~30 months after hatching; first-year mortality can reach 50–60%.
- A small farm can't compete on meat; the visitor offer must carry it.
- Feather/welfare scrutiny is rising — design for high welfare and say so.
- Al Ain ostrich farming was tried by several small farms from 1997; only one is visibly scaled. Plan for a 5–7 month visitor season.

## What this means for the empire
- Keep the venture at **Idea/Validate**; spend nothing beyond enquiries and a site visit until gates 1–2 are answered in writing.
- Reframe the concept as an **agri-tourism destination with ostriches**, ideally on an Emirati partner's licensed farm or in partnership with Ostrich Oasis, rather than a production farm.
- Next actions, all near-free: (1) MOCCAE written enquiry on South African, Namibian and hatching-egg imports; (2) ADAFSA enquiry via TAMM for the full 145-activity list, the ratite question and whether a licensed establishment can operate on a partner's farm; (3) ask ADAFSA/Ma'an how farm owners and operators join **Nama** — being the operator for a Nama farm could replace buying or leasing land; (4) visit Ostrich Oasis and ask for its visitor numbers, prices and survival data.
- Design the offer as **tour + ostrich meal + shop + optional farm stay** (all four lines have a licensing precedent or an international comparable), with a small display flock — not a breeding farm.
- Size any first commitment as a small, staged bet ([[Kelly Criterion and Position Sizing]]) — the downside is a sunk build on a farm plot that can be repossessed.
- Expect losses for 3+ years — don't elect Small Business Relief in loss years so losses carry forward ([[UAE Corporate Tax Essentials]]).
- Abu Dhabi farm tenure differs from Dubai freehold; structure separately ([[UAE Holding Structures]]).

## Open questions
- ADAFSA position on ratites and non-national farm leases; DMT rules on expatriate Izab tenancy and subletting.
- The full list of the 145 farm activities (Decision 5/2025) and what a "licensed establishment" needs to operate on someone else's farm.
- Nama: eligibility, application route and whether outside operators can partner with selected farms.
- MOCCAE current status on South African birds (2019 ban) and hatching eggs; Namibia as an alternative origin.
- Ratite feed price delivered to Al Ain (the AED 1.65/kg Fodder Market average is a floor).
- Ostrich Oasis's visitor offer and prices.
- Abu Dhabi licensing for on-farm abattoir, butchery and restaurant.
- Current SA producer prices (R/kg meat, per skin) and feather yield per bird (Farmer's Weekly, Jan 2025, describes meat prices only as "fair"; DALRRD's value-chain profile blocks automated access).
- Visitor pricing and footfall at Al Ain farm attractions.
