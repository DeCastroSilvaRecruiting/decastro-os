# UAE Corporate Tax Essentials

> **Bottom line:** Small ventures will usually pay little or no Corporate Tax: 0% on the first AED 375,000 of taxable profit, 9% above, and Small Business Relief (treat as zero taxable income if revenue ≤ AED 3m) extends to tax periods ending on or before **31 Dec 2029** (MD 131/2026, announced 7 Aug 2026; reconfirmed Nov 2026). But every taxable entity must still **register within 3 months of incorporation, file a return within 9 months of year end and keep records for 7 years** — the penalties for missing paperwork (AED 10,000 each for late registration and poor records) dwarf the tax. From **1 Jul 2027** businesses under AED 50m revenue must also e-invoice B2B sales, with new penalties for non-compliance (updated Nov 2026). Not advice — confirm with a licensed UAE tax adviser.

## Context
Decision informed: how to set up and run DCS Golf, Wednesday Circuit and the Al Ain Ostrich Farm — as personal (sole) businesses or companies, mainland or free zone — and what compliance calendar to keep. Pairs with [[UAE Holding Structures]].

## Rates (as of Nov 2026)
| Taxable income | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Up to AED 375,000 | 0% | Decree-Law 47/2022 Art. 3 + Cabinet Decision 116/2022 [1] |
| Above AED 375,000 | 9% | [1] |
| Qualifying income of a Qualifying Free Zone Person | 0% | Art. 18 [1] |
| MNE groups ≥ EUR 750m revenue (in 2 of the last 4 years) | Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax to a 15% minimum effective rate (Cabinet Decision 142/2024, per EY [21]; 15% not stated on the MoF page [5]) for financial years starting on or after 1 Jan 2025 | [5][21] — not relevant to the owner |

No change to the 0%/9% rates or the AED 375,000 band was found in searches of MoF and FTA announcements through Nov 2026.

**New in 2026 — R&D tax credit (Tier 2 only):** for tax periods starting on or after 1 Jan 2026, juridical persons doing pre-approved R&D in the UAE can claim a non-refundable credit of 15–50% of qualifying spend, with a minimum of AED 500,000 per project per year; entities that elect SBR are excluded (Cabinet Decision 215/2025 and MD 24/2026, per Deloitte [22]; Tier 1 text not read). Not relevant to the ventures at current scale (updated Nov 2026).

## Who is taxable
- **Companies** (mainland and free zone): taxable persons from incorporation.
- **Individuals doing business** (sole establishments, freelancers): taxable only if **business revenue from all their business activities exceeds AED 1,000,000 in a calendar year** (Cabinet Decision 49/2023, Art. 2 [2]; reconfirmed on the FTA registration page, Nov 2026 [14]). Wages, personal investment income and real estate investment income are excluded at any amount [2][14]; property income stays excluded only while no licence is required (a holiday-home permit counts as a licence; Ejari does not) [13].
- Implication: one person running three ventures as sole establishments has **one** AED 1m test across all three.

## Registration (as of Nov 2026)
- Companies (resident juridical persons, including free zone) formed on or after 1 Mar 2024: register within **3 months** of incorporation (FTA Decision 3/2024) [9][15].
- Resident individuals: register by **31 March** of the year after the year turnover first exceeded AED 1m [9] (the FTA's own announcement states this explicitly only for 2024 [15]; PwC reads it as applying to later years too).
- Late registration: **AED 10,000** [4][14]. FTA waiver initiative (reconfirmed Nov 2026): waived automatically if the first return (or annual declaration) is filed within **7 months** of the end of the first tax period; already-paid penalties credited to EmaraTax [6][14]. No end date stated — don't rely on it.

## Small Business Relief (SBR)
- Election available if revenue is **≤ AED 3m** in the current period *and every previous period* (MD 73/2023) [3].
- Available for tax periods ending **on or before 31 Dec 2029** (MD 131/2026, which amends the SBR ministerial decision; the MoF announcement keeps the AED 3m threshold and states no other changed conditions — as of Nov 2026) [3][8]. Applies to 2026.
- **Not available to** Qualifying Free Zone Persons or members of MNE groups.
- Cost of electing: that year's tax losses and net interest cannot be carried forward; and an SBR entity cannot claim the new R&D credit [22].
- **Anti-fragmentation:** if a business is artificially split and combined revenue exceeds AED 3m, the FTA can deny relief under the general anti-abuse rule (Art. 50). Separate ventures with genuinely different businesses (golf goods, events, farming) should be distinguishable — judgement; confirm with an adviser.
- Electing still requires registration, a (simplified) return and records [7].

## Free zone companies — Qualifying Free Zone Person (QFZP)
Conditions (Art. 18 [1], Cabinet Decision 100/2023 [16]): adequate substance in the free zone; earn qualifying income; no election for the standard rate; transfer-pricing compliance; **audited financial statements** (MD 84/2025) [11]; non-qualifying revenue within the **de minimis** limit. Cabinet Decision 100/2023 Art. 4(1) sets the test as the lower of a percentage of total revenue or an amount, both "specified by the Minister" [16]; the commonly cited figures — 5% or AED 5m — come from Tier 2 summaries, and the Ministerial Decision that sets them was not verified this run. Qualifying activities are now set by **MD 229/2025** (issued 28 Aug 2025, retroactive to 1 Jun 2023), which repealed MD 265/2023 [10][23]. Losing status reportedly lasts for the period plus the next four (not confirmed in the law text read this run).

Judgement: a QFZP selling to UAE mainland consumers (DCS Golf DTC, Wednesday Circuit corporate clients) will mostly earn non-qualifying income — the free zone 0% rate rarely helps a local consumer business; SBR and the AED 375k band do more.

## Filing, payment, records
- Return **and payment** within **9 months** of the end of the tax period (Art. 53) [1][7].
- Records retained **7 years** (Art. 56) [1]. From 2026 the Tax Procedures rules add two years where a timely refund application is still pending (per Deloitte [20]; confirm) (updated Nov 2026).
- **Refunds (new from 1 Jan 2026):** a refund or use of a credit balance must be requested within **5 years** of the end of the relevant tax period (Federal Decree-Law 17/2025 amending the Tax Procedures Law) [19]. The MoF announcement does not list tax types; the Tax Procedures Law governs Corporate Tax administration, so treat it as applying — confirm with an adviser (updated Nov 2026).
- Related-party / connected-person disclosure may be required with the return; master/local file only above thresholds (Art. 55) [1]. Payments to the owner (salary, rent, loans) are connected-person transactions — document them at arm's length.
- Audited accounts: required above AED 50m revenue, for tax groups, and for QFZPs (MD 84/2025) [11].
- Tax group: parent must hold ≥ 95% of capital, votes, profits and net assets (Art. 40) [1].

## E-invoicing (as of Nov 2026)
- **Businesses below AED 50m revenue:** appoint an Accredited Service Provider (ASP) by **31 Mar 2027** and go live by **1 Jul 2027**; voluntary onboarding from 1 Jul 2026; penalties apply only from the mandatory date (MoF e-invoicing guidelines V2.0, 26 Oct 2026) [17]. Unchanged.
- **Supplies to consumers (B2C) are outside scope** [17].
- Businesses at or above AED 50m: ASP deadline moved from 31 Jul 2026 to **30 Oct 2026**; go-live still 1 Jan 2027 (MD 66/2026, amending MD 244/2025) [18] (updated Nov 2026) — not relevant to the ventures.
- MD 197/2026 (effective 1 Oct 2026) amends MD 243/2025: transactions between members of the same Tax Group are excluded for 24 months from 1 Jan 2027; the requirement to store e-invoice data inside the UAE was removed (keep it for the Tax Procedures Law period instead) [24] (updated Nov 2026).
- **New penalties (Cabinet Decision 173/2026)** [25] (updated Nov 2026):

| E-invoicing violation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Not implementing on time (including not appointing an ASP) | AED 5,000, plus AED 5,000 per month it continues; max AED 50,000 in any 12 months |
| Not issuing/transmitting an e-invoice or e-credit note | AED 100 each, max AED 5,000 per month |
| Not telling the ASP about changes to registered data | AED 250 per day, max AED 5,000 per month |

The decision text published by MoF has blank issue dates and comes into force the day after Official Gazette publication; the gazette date was not verified [25].

## Key Corporate Tax penalties (Cabinet Decision 75/2023 as amended, Mar 2024 consolidation; reconfirmed as unchanged Nov 2026) [4]
| Violation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Late registration | AED 10,000 |
| Failure to keep records | AED 10,000 (AED 20,000 repeat within 24 months) |
| Late return | AED 500/month for 12 months, then AED 1,000/month |
| Late payment | 14% per year, monthly |
| Late deregistration | AED 1,000/month, max AED 10,000 |

Cabinet Decision 129/2025 (in force 14 Apr 2026) reformed **VAT and excise** penalties, largely aligning them with the Corporate Tax regime (e.g. late payment now 14% a year for VAT too); no amendment to the Corporate Tax penalty decision was found in MoF or FTA sources through Nov 2026 [20] (updated Nov 2026).

## Worked example (illustrative AED)
Three ventures in their second year:

| Scenario | Revenue | Taxable profit | Tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. One LLC per venture, each revenue < 3m, SBR elected | 0.8m / 1.2m / 0.6m | 150k / 300k / –50k | **0** (but the farm's loss can't be carried forward in an SBR year) |
| B. Same, no SBR | same | 150k / 300k / –50k | 0 / 0 / 0 — each under AED 375k anyway; farm keeps its loss |
| C. All three as owner's sole establishments | 2.6m combined (> AED 1m → taxable) | 400k combined | SBR elected → 0; without SBR (400k − 375k) × 9% = **AED 2,250** |
| D. One LLC, profit 900k, revenue 3.5m | 3.5m | 900k | (900k − 375k) × 9% = **AED 47,250**; SBR unavailable |

Lessons: (1) while profits are small, the AED 375k band alone usually brings tax to zero, so **don't elect SBR for a loss-making venture** (Al Ain farm, early years) — keep the loss; (2) separate companies each get their own AED 375k band — but the FTA can challenge splits made only for tax.

## Key takeaways
- 0% to AED 375k, 9% above; SBR (≤ AED 3m revenue) runs to tax periods ending 31 Dec 2029 — no rate changes in 2026.
- Sole traders: AED 1m combined-turnover trigger across all their businesses.
- Register within 3 months of incorporation; file and pay within 9 months; keep records 7 years; claim refunds within 5 years.
- Free zone 0% needs audited accounts, substance and qualifying income — rarely useful for local B2C.
- E-invoicing for small businesses: appoint an ASP by 31 Mar 2027, live by 1 Jul 2027; missing it costs AED 5,000 a month up to AED 50,000.

## What this means for the empire
- **DCS Golf / Wednesday Circuit:** set up as companies (limited liability matters more than tax at this size); register for CT within 3 months; consider SBR each year only where profitable. Wednesday Circuit's corporate (B2B) sales will need e-invoicing from 1 Jul 2027; DCS Golf's direct-to-consumer sales are outside scope, but its wholesale/corporate orders are not.
- **Al Ain Ostrich Farm:** greenfield losses are valuable — do **not** elect SBR in loss years; keep audited-quality books from day one.
- **Calendar:** put CT registration, the 9-month return/payment deadline, the e-invoicing ASP deadline (31 Mar 2027) and go-live (1 Jul 2027) into the app's compliance tracker.
- Keep personal rental property unlicensed and personal to stay outside CT (see [[UAE Holding Structures]]).
- A licensed UAE tax adviser should confirm: SBR anti-fragmentation risk across the three ventures, sole-establishment vs company choice, e-invoicing scope for each venture, and any QFZP plans.
