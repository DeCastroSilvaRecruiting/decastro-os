# UAE E-commerce Seller Set-up: Licence, VAT and Corporate Tax

> **Bottom line:** For a Dubai-based private-label seller whose customers are consumers on Amazon.ae, the free zone 0% Corporate Tax rate is effectively unavailable: sales to natural persons are an *excluded activity* under the qualifying-activities decision, and the free zone route adds a mainland-access and customs layer for no tax gain. A **Dubai mainland (DET) LLC with an online-retail activity**, a Dubai Customs code, Corporate Tax registration within 3 months and VAT registration on time is the cleanest year-one set-up. At AED 400k–600k revenue the Corporate Tax bill is **nil either way** (AED 375k zero band), so choose on liability, Amazon and bank acceptance, and running cost, not on tax.

*Confidence is medium. The rules on natural persons, Small Business Relief (SBR), VAT thresholds and customs registration come from Tier 1 pages. But the current MD 229/2025 PDF could not be fetched this run (its predecessor MD 265/2023 was read, and secondary sources say the exception list is unchanged). Licence fees are mostly from providers' own pages or Tier 3 firms. Amazon's payout currency for Amazon.ae was not confirmed on an Amazon page.*

## What Amazon and Customs require (Fact)
- **Amazon.ae:** a business operating in the UAE needs a valid commercial licence, "whether you operate from mainland UAE or a freezone" [25]. Registration also needs a national ID (for example Emirates ID), proof of address, and a bank account for proceeds. A power of attorney is needed if a representative registers [24]. Amazon pays by bank transfer (ACH/EFT), taking up to five business days, and **will not pay to a credit card or PayPal** [24]. Professional accounts have no monthly fee "for a limited time" (as of Nov 2026) [24].
- **DET x Amazon programme (2026):** sellers with a "Dubai Trade License" can get up to **AED 2,000 cashback on ad spend** (100% of monthly ad spend), from 1 Jan to 31 Dec 2026. Emirati sellers get extra benefits [26]. Judgement: "Dubai Trade License" most likely means a DET licence, not a free zone one. Confirm with Amazon.
- **Importer code:** Amazon does not act as importer of record. The seller, or its broker, files the entry and pays duty and import VAT ([28], T3, consistent with [[Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics]]). Dubai Customs registration ("Request Business Registration") needs a trade licence copy, a passport copy and an Emirates ID copy. It costs **AED 100 + AED 20** knowledge fee, is issued in 1 working day and renews for AED 25 (Service Guide v9, 2025) [22]. The guide does not set a separate code type for free zone companies [22][23].
- **Free zone stock going to the mainland:** Dubai Customs says that for a free zone company "an appropriate customs declaration should be processed for sale of goods to mainland" [23]. The tariff lists an "Import to Local from FZ" declaration at AED 70 (dutiable) or AED 80 (non-dutiable) [22]. Duty of 5% and import VAT are due when goods leave the zone for UAE circulation ([28], T3).
- **TDRA:** all e-commerce licences need approval from the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority. Some emirates add an e-commerce activity to a normal commercial licence [14].

## Corporate Tax: can B2C Amazon sales get the free zone 0%?
**Short answer: no, not in any meaningful amount (Fact on the rule; Judgement on how it applies to Amazon).**

- **Excluded activity.** The predecessor decision, MD 265/2023, lists as excluded "any transactions with natural persons, except transactions in relation to" four qualifying activities: ships, fund management, wealth and investment management, and aircraft financing and leasing [2]. MD 229/2025 repealed and replaced MD 265 with effect from 1 Jun 2023 [3][4]. IFZA's 2026 guide still lists "transactions with natural persons, except some specific transactions related to Qualifying Activities" as excluded [5]. A Tier 3 summary of MD 229 Art 2(2)(a) gives the same four carve-backs [7]. **The current MD 229 text could not be fetched this run, so re-read it before relying on this.**
- **Distribution does not rescue it.** "Distribution of goods or materials in or from a Designated Zone" qualifies only if the goods enter the UAE through the Designated Zone and are supplied to a customer who **resells, processes or alters them**, or to a public benefit entity [3][6]. An Amazon shopper is an end user. FTA Decision 6/2026 also requires a QFZP distributor to obtain an **agreed-upon-procedures report** each period, for tax periods starting on or after 1 Jan 2026; failing to file it costs QFZP status for that period and the next four [6].
- **De minimis.** Non-qualifying revenue must stay within the lower of 5% of revenue or AED 5m [7]. A store whose sales are almost all to consumers breaches this on day one.
- **No SBR for QFZPs.** SBR is not available to a Qualifying Free Zone Person [9].
- **Consequence (Judgement):** a free zone e-commerce company selling B2C will not be a QFZP. It is taxed at the standard rate (0% to AED 375k, 9% above), and because it is not a QFZP, it should be able to elect SBR like a mainland company. **Adviser to confirm** that a free zone person which fails the QFZP conditions can elect SBR in that period.
- **Amazon as the customer?** Amazon's VAT treatment of a marketplace as an "undisclosed agent" (buyer from the seller) applies to platforms that act in their own name ([13], T3). Amazon.ae's seller agreement treats the seller as seller of record (see [[Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics]]). Judgement: do not build a structure on the idea that Amazon is the "reseller customer". Ask the adviser only if a wholesale-to-Amazon (vendor) model is ever considered.

## Small Business Relief (Fact)
- Elect to be treated as having no taxable income if revenue is **≤ AED 3m**. The threshold applies from 1 Jun 2023 and, under **MD 131 (announced 7 Aug 2026)**, for tax periods ending **on or before 31 Dec 2029** [8].
- Excluded: QFZPs and members of MNE groups with consolidated revenue above AED 3.15bn. Tax losses and disallowed net interest from an SBR year **cannot be carried forward**. The anti-abuse rule (Art 50) applies to artificial splitting [9].
- Revenue above AED 3m in any earlier period ends eligibility (per [[UAE Corporate Tax Essentials]]). Registration, a return and records are still required.

## VAT (Fact unless marked)
- **Mandatory registration:** when **taxable supplies and imports** in the past 12 months exceed **AED 375,000**, or are expected to in the next 30 days. **Voluntary:** above AED 187,500 of supplies, imports *or taxable expenses* [10]. Imports count towards the test, so an importer can reach the threshold before sales reach AED 375k. Adviser to confirm how imports are measured.
- **Timing:** apply **within 30 days** of being required to register. Documents: trade licence, owners' Emirates ID and passports, customs information if applicable, and a bank letter in the company's name (optional). The FTA's processing estimate is 20 business days; registration is free [11].
- **Penalty for late registration:** **AED 10,000**, unchanged under Cabinet Decision 129/2025, effective 14 Apr 2026. Late returns cost AED 1,000 (AED 2,000 on a repeat within 24 months). Late payment is now 14% a year (≈ 1.167% a month) [12].
- **Who accounts for VAT:** the seller, on the gross VAT-inclusive price. The UAE has no marketplace deemed-supplier rule for this model ([28], T3; consistent with the FBA article). Box 1 reporting by emirate of receipt applies only above AED 100m of e-commerce supplies (MD 26/2023 per [13], T3). Not relevant at this scale.
- **Import VAT:** 5% on CIF + duty. A registrant whose TRN is linked to its customs code self-accounts, so it nets to zero; otherwise it is paid in cash at clearance ([28], T3).
- **Is early voluntary registration worth it? (Judgement):** not automatically. For a B2C seller, being unregistered means import VAT and VAT on fees are a cost, but the seller keeps the 5% output VAT that a registrant would pay over. Below the threshold, staying unregistered is usually cheaper (see worked example). Register voluntarily only if year-one sales will clearly cross AED 375k anyway, to avoid a mid-year switch and repricing.

## Worked example (illustrative AED, year one, mainland LLC)
Assumptions (judgement, not quotes): consumer prices are VAT-inclusive; landed stock CIF is 35% of net sales, plus 5% duty; Amazon referral and FBA fees are 25% of gross. Whether Amazon.ae charges UAE VAT on its fees is **unverified**, so 5% VAT on fees is assumed and shown separately.

**Scenario 1: AED 400,000 net sales (gross AED 420,000), profit AED 60,000**
| Item | AED |
|---|---|
| Imports CIF (35% × 400k) | 140,000 |
| Supplies + imports for the VAT test | 540,000, so registration is **mandatory**, likely by mid-year |
| Output VAT (5% × 400k) | 20,000 |
| Import VAT recoverable (5% × (140k + 7k duty)) | (7,350) |
| VAT on Amazon fees recoverable (5% × 25% × 420k), if charged | (5,250) |
| **Net VAT payable to FTA** | **≈ 7,400** (≈ 12,650 if no VAT on fees) |
| Corporate Tax on AED 60k profit | **0** (inside the AED 375k band; SBR unnecessary) |

**Scenario 2: AED 600,000 revenue, AED 150,000 profit**
| Structure | Corporate Tax | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Mainland LLC, no SBR | **0** | 150k < 375k band [31]; keeps any loss for later years |
| Mainland LLC, SBR elected | **0** | No gain from electing; simpler return but losses forfeited |
| Free zone company claiming QFZP | Not a QFZP: 600k non-qualifying against a de minimis cap of 30k (5%), so taxed as standard = **0** | Same tax, extra licence/customs layer; would also have needed audited accounts as a QFZP |
| eTrader (individual) | **0**, and no CT registration while the owner's combined business turnover ≤ AED 1m [31] | At 600k plus any other personal business turnover, check the AED 1m test |

**Where it starts to matter: Scenario 3, AED 2.8m revenue, AED 500,000 profit**
- No SBR: (500,000 − 375,000) × 9% = **AED 11,250**.
- SBR elected (revenue ≤ 3m): **AED 0**.
- Once revenue exceeds AED 3m in any year, SBR is gone for good, and the bill becomes AED 11,250 a year at this profit.

Lesson: in years one and two, the licence fee difference between routes (≈ AED 1,400 to ≈ AED 25,000 including a DET permit) is far larger than any tax difference. Tax becomes material only when profit passes AED 375k.

## Banking and payouts
- **Fact:** Amazon pays only to a bank account set as the deposit method, by ACH/EFT, in up to 5 business days [24]. Marketplaces expect a **UAE corporate account in the licensed entity's name** ([29], T3). The FTA's VAT form asks for a bank letter in the company's name [11].
- **Fact:** Amazon's Currency Converter for Sellers supports **AED payouts at a flat 1.5%** fee. This matters if the store later sells on Amazon.com, .co.uk or .sa into an AED account [27].
- **Judgement (unverified):** Amazon.ae settles in AED to a UAE IBAN. Confirm in Seller Central under "Deposit methods" before opening accounts.
- **USD side (Judgement):** Chinese factories invoice in USD. Hold an AED operating account plus a USD sub-account at the same bank, so supplier payments avoid two conversions. The AED is pegged at 3.6725 per USD (see [[Treasury Liquidity Tiers]]), so FX risk is mostly in the bank's spread, not the rate. Get spread and transfer-fee quotes from two UAE banks and one licensed payment provider.
- **Opening the account:** expect banks to ask for a lease (Ejari or flexi-desk), the licence, a business plan, supplier and Amazon evidence, and the UBO's source of funds (judgement, consistent with [[UAE Holding Structures]]). A mainland LLC with a visible Dubai address is the easier case.

## Recommended year-one structure (Judgement)
1. **Entity:** a **Dubai mainland LLC**, 100% owned by the owner. If the ADGM/DIFC holdco from [[UAE Holding Structures]] already exists, the holdco should own the LLC from day one; otherwise do not form a holdco just for this store. Activities: online retail of the store's product categories, plus general trading or import, so the customs code covers the products. If Dubai CommerCity's "Dual License with DET" costs clearly less all-in, it is an acceptable alternative.
2. **Not a free zone company to chase 0%.** B2C income cannot qualify, and the mainland-access layer adds cost and customs steps.
3. **Not an eTrader for the real business.** Use it only for a very small test, and only if the owner is eligible. It means unlimited liability and a shared AED 1m personal turnover test.
4. **Within the first 3 months:** Dubai Customs code (AED 120, 1 day), Corporate Tax registration (AED 10,000 penalty if late), Amazon Professional account, UAE bank account in the company's name.
5. **VAT:** monitor *supplies + imports* monthly. Apply within 30 days of the forecast or actual crossing of AED 375k. Link the TRN to the customs code so import VAT is self-accounted.
6. **Corporate Tax:** standard regime. Do not elect SBR in a loss year. Consider SBR when profit approaches AED 375k and revenue stays ≤ AED 3m.
7. **Books:** inventory-based accrual accounts from day one (landed cost per SKU, Amazon settlement reconciliation, gross sales for VAT). Software must be ready for e-invoicing in 2027 (B2C currently out of scope; see [[UAE Corporate Tax Essentials]]).

## Key takeaways
- Amazon.ae accepts mainland or free zone licences. Customs registration costs AED 120 and takes a day; Amazon is never the importer.
- B2C sales are an excluded activity for the free zone 0% rate, so a consumer store gets no free zone tax benefit.
- At AED 400k–600k revenue, Corporate Tax is nil in every structure. Tax matters only once profit passes AED 375k, and SBR (≤ AED 3m, to 2029) covers that gap.
- VAT counts **supplies plus imports**: an importer likely crosses AED 375k in year one. Register within 30 days or face a AED 10,000 penalty.
- A mainland DET LLC is the simplest compliant vehicle. A free zone setup only adds a mainland-access layer.

## What this means for the empire
- **Global Trend Store:** set up a mainland DET LLC (or a Dubai CommerCity dual licence if cheaper) before the first purchase order. Budget about **AED 15,000–20,000** for year-one set-up (estimate, T3 figures plus a flexi-desk allowance). Add a CT registration task (3 months) and a VAT watch (supplies + imports) to the app's compliance tracker.
- **DCS Golf:** the same logic applies to its direct-to-consumer online sales. If DCS Golf already has a mainland entity, adding an online-retail activity to it may be cheaper than a second company. But SBR's anti-fragmentation rule and separate-brand risk argue for keeping them apart. Ask the adviser.
- **Holding structure:** this LLC should sit under the venture holdco proposed in [[UAE Holding Structures]] once it exists. Never put the store inside a free zone entity that depends on 0% status: its B2C revenue would breach that entity's de minimis limit.
- **Cash:** VAT and duty are cash costs at the border until the TRN is linked. Plan the first orders with that 10.25% cash outlay in [[Treasury Liquidity Tiers]].
