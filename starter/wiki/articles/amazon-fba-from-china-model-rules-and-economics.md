# Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics

> **Bottom line:** The version of "buy trending items from China and sell on Amazon" that survives is **private label through Fulfilment by Amazon (FBA)**, with your company as the seller of record, a UAE trade licence and a customs client code. Amazon's own Amazon.ae rate card now confirms the fees: 15% referral on home, kitchen and similar categories, FBA from AED 5.5 a unit, AED 2 per cubic foot per month storage, all **before 5% VAT on the fees**. Input can fall to a few hours a week once delegated, but the business is **front-loaded, ties up cash in stock for months, and loses most launches**. Treat it as a portfolio of small, killable bets, not as passive income on day one.

*Confidence is medium. The Nov 2026 review (see Verification log) confirmed the referral, FBA and storage fees, customs duty, VAT thresholds, import VAT handling and the corporate tax lines against Tier 1 pages. The Seller Central help pages for the trade-licence rule, the full drop shipping policy and the Brand Registry list of trademark offices could not be fetched, and the normal monthly subscription is not published. Those items stay secondary.*

## Context
The owner wants an online store that links trending products from reputable Chinese sellers to audiences worldwide, with as little ongoing input as possible. This article records what the model is allowed to be, what it costs, and where it breaks. It informs the venture **Global Trend Store** in De Castro OS and the Commerce Lab tools.

## Which models are allowed
| Model | How it works | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Private label + FBA** | Factory makes a product to your spec under your brand; you import it to Amazon's warehouse; Amazon picks, packs, ships, handles returns and customer service | Compliant. Best fit for low input. Highest capital and design effort. |
| **Wholesale / retail arbitrage** | Buy branded stock from authorised sources and resell | Compliant, but brand permissions and thin margins |
| **Drop shipping where the supplier ships a branded parcel** | Customer orders on Amazon; supplier posts its own parcel | **Not allowed.** Amazon permits drop shipping only under its Drop Shipping Policy. |

Amazon's own explainer (Aug 2025, [4]) says a drop-shipping seller must be the **seller of record** (owning the goods before the customer buys), must keep packaging, packing slips and invoices **free of any reference that identifies a third party**, and must accept and process returns. It does not list the penalties. The policy text itself (Seller Central, reference 201808410) could not be fetched in this review. The statement that violations can restrict fulfilment or the account comes from a vendor summary [15] and is unverified.

## What you need in the UAE
- **A valid UAE trade licence for Amazon.ae.** Reported by a setup firm [14] (July 2026), including that sellers registered with ID only had until the end of 2025 to comply. **Unverified**: the Seller Central help pages were not reachable and Amazon's public Middle East page does not mention a licence [3]. Treat it as required in practice: Dubai Customs also requires one (next bullet).
- A licence activity covering online retail. Indicative costs: free zone e-commerce from about AED 5,750; mainland Dubai from about AED 15,000; the home-based DED Trader licence is a poor fit for FBA. These are one vendor's numbers (July 2026, [14]), unverified. See [[UAE E-commerce Seller Set-up: Licence, VAT and Corporate Tax]].
- **A customs client code.** Dubai Customs policy: every natural or legal person dealing with the customs administration must register to get a customs code; the code follows the activities on the licence and expires with it [7]. Registration needs a copy of a valid trade licence and the authorised person's passport [6]. That Amazon is not the importer of record is from vendor sources [14][16] and unverified at Tier 1.
- **Brand Registry:** free [2][5]. Needs an active registered trademark or a pending application, as a word mark or a design mark with words, letters or numbers, from an Amazon-approved government IP office; a pending application filed through Amazon IP Accelerator qualifies [5]. **Whether a UAE Ministry of Economy trademark is on Amazon's approved list is unverified**: the eligibility page could not be fetched.

All Amazon fees are **exclusive of VAT, which is added on top** [1]. A VAT-registered seller recovers that VAT as input tax; an unregistered seller bears it.

## Import and tax (UAE)
- **Customs duty:** the GCC common customs tariff is **5% of CIF value** for goods from outside the customs union [6]. Some categories differ. Customs value is the transaction value plus transport and insurance to the GCC border [7].
- **Import VAT:** **VAT-registered importers do not pay at the border.** They account for import VAT in the VAT return for the period of import [9], and normally recover it in the same return, so it is close to cash-neutral. *Corrected from the first version, which put the border cash cost at about 10.25% of value.* That figure (5% duty plus 5% VAT on value plus duty) applies only to **unregistered** importers, who pay VAT at customs through the FTA portal [9]; the "value plus duty" VAT base comes from vendor sources and was not confirmed at Tier 1 because the VAT law PDF could not be fetched.
- **VAT registration:** mandatory when taxable supplies and imports exceed **AED 375,000** over the past 12 months or are expected to within 30 days; voluntary above **AED 187,500** (supplies and imports, or expenses) [8] (page updated Apr 2026). That the seller, not Amazon, accounts for VAT on its sales is a vendor view [14]; the FTA e-commerce VAT guide could not be fetched. Judgement: register voluntarily before the first import so import VAT and the VAT on Amazon fees can be recovered. Counterpoint from [[UAE E-commerce Seller Set-up: Licence, VAT and Corporate Tax]]: for a pure B2C seller below the threshold, registering means charging 5% output VAT on every sale, which usually costs more than the recovered input VAT; and imports count towards the AED 375,000 test, so many first-year sellers cross it anyway. Decide with the adviser on projected numbers.
- **Corporate tax:** 0% on taxable income up to **AED 375,000** and **9%** above [10] (as of Oct 2026). **Small Business Relief**: an eligible resident with revenue of no more than **AED 3 million** in the period and all prior periods can elect to be treated as having no taxable income, for tax periods ending on or before **31 Dec 2029** (Ministerial Decision 131 of 2026, announced 7 Aug 2026 [11]). A return must still be filed [12].
- **Free zone 0% rate:** Ministerial Decision 229 of 2025 makes "any transactions with natural persons" an **excluded activity**, with exceptions only for shipping, fund management, wealth management and aircraft leasing [13]. General goods trading is not a qualifying activity; distribution from a Designated Zone qualifies only when the buyer resells or processes the goods [13]. Judgement: Amazon sales to consumers look like transactions with natural persons, so they would not get the 0% rate. **A licensed tax adviser must confirm this before the entity is chosen**: see [[UAE Corporate Tax Essentials]] and [[UAE Holding Structures]].

## Economics: a worked example (illustrative, not a quote)
A small, light home product (standard parcel, up to 0.5 kg), VAT-registered, Amazon.ae. Updated Nov 2026: the FBA fee now uses Amazon's rate card instead of an assumption.

| Per unit | AED |
|---|---|
| Shelf price (VAT-inclusive) | 99.00 |
| Net revenue after 5% VAT (99 ÷ 1.05) | 94.29 |
| Factory price (about USD 4.1) | 15.00 |
| Freight to FBA | 3.00 |
| Inspection | 0.50 |
| Customs duty 5% on CIF (15 + 3) [6] | 0.90 |
| **Landed cost** | **19.40** |
| Referral fee 15% of 99 (Home) [1] | 14.85 |
| FBA fee, standard parcel ≤0.5 kg, price above AED 25 [1] | 9.50 *(was 9.00 assumed)* |
| Storage and returns allowance | 1.14 |
| **Contribution before ads** | **49.40** *(was 49.90)* |
| Advertising at 15% of price | 14.85 |
| **Net profit per unit** | **34.55** *(was 35.05)*: 37% of net revenue, 178% return on landed stock cost |

**Freight warning (Nov 2026):** the AED 3.00 freight line predates the 2026 Strait of Hormuz closure. [[China Sourcing Playbook for UAE Private-Label Importers]] puts rerouted sea freight at about AED 5 a unit for small orders; at AED 5 the duty rises to AED 1.00, landed cost to AED 21.50 and net profit to about AED 32.45 a unit. Use live forwarder quotes.

Notes: Amazon's fees carry 5% VAT on top [1]; that is recovered by a registered seller and so left out above. An unregistered seller would pay about AED 1.22 more per unit (5% of 14.85 + 9.50) and could not recover import VAT. Judgement: the referral base is the VAT-inclusive shelf price, because Amazon charges on the total price the customer pays; confirm against a Seller Central fee preview.

This is a best-case teaching example. Real products often land closer to 10 to 20% margin after ads, returns and price competition. The Commerce Lab calculator reruns this with your own quotes; the rule of thumb in the app is **revenue at least 3x landed cost and 25% net margin after ads**.

## Cash is the real constraint
Stock is paid for 2 to 4 months before it sells. Illustrative simulation using the example product above (run on the first version at AED 35.05 a unit; the AED 0.50 fee change moves the results by under 2%, so they are not rerun here): eight launches, one every two months; AED 12,000 first order and AED 3,000 launch advertising per launch; a 30% hit rate; winners reach 200 units a month over four months; two-month order-to-shelf lead time; 40% of a failed first order recovered; AED 1,500 a month overhead. Result: **peak cash need about AED 40,000, cumulative profit turning positive around month 16, and about AED 15,000 a month of profit by month 24**. At a 15% hit rate the same programme needs about AED 62,000 of peak cash and only just breaks even around month 24. Failed launches cost the first order and launch advertising, which is why the model assumes most launches fail. Rerun it with your own numbers in the Cash forecast tab.

## Where it breaks
- **Product selection:** hype products with weak margins, heavy competition or seasonality.
- **Compliance and IP:** branded or patented designs, gated categories, products needing safety certification (batteries, electrical, children's, cosmetics, food contact). Check UAE and GCC conformity rules by product type.
- **Reviews:** only Amazon-approved programmes; incentivised or fake reviews end accounts.
- **Cash:** stock-outs hurt rank; overstock burns cash, and stock held over 365 days attracts an unpublished long-term storage fee [1].
- **Fees drift:** Amazon changes fees (last FBA revision Aug 2025) and the zero subscription is a limited-time offer.
- **Single-platform risk:** one suspension stops the income. Build your own store and Noon alongside.

## Key takeaways
- Be the seller of record; private label with FBA is the compliant, low-input route.
- Amazon's own rate card confirms the fee stack: about 15% referral plus AED 7–22 FBA per standard unit plus storage, all plus 5% VAT.
- Kill products on maths: 3x price-to-cost and 25% net margin after ads, no red flags.
- Register for VAT before the first import: import VAT then goes through the return instead of the border, and Amazon's VAT on fees is recoverable.
- Budget for months of tied-up cash and for most launches to fail; get an adviser's view on corporate tax and the free zone question before choosing the entity.
