# Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics

> **Bottom line:** The version of "buy trending items from China and sell on Amazon" that survives is **private label through Fulfilment by Amazon (FBA)**, with your company as the seller of record, a UAE trade licence and an importer code. Input can fall to a few hours a week once suppliers, freight, listings and ads are delegated or automated, but the business is **front-loaded, ties up cash in stock for months, and loses most launches**. Treat it as a portfolio of small, killable bets, not as passive income on day one.

*Confidence is medium: the fee and tax figures come mostly from a setup-services firm and a freight firm, plus one Amazon page without rates. Amazon's own Seller Central pages and the Federal Tax Authority must confirm every number before money moves. A queued review item does this.*

## Context
The owner wants an online store that links trending products from reputable Chinese sellers to audiences worldwide, with as little ongoing input as possible. This article records what the model is allowed to be, what it costs, and where it breaks. It informs the venture **Global Trend Store** in De Castro OS and the Commerce Lab tools.

## Which models are allowed
| Model | How it works | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Private label + FBA** | Factory makes a product to your spec under your brand; you import it to Amazon's warehouse; Amazon picks, packs, ships, handles returns and customer service | Compliant. Best fit for low input. Highest capital and design effort. |
| **Wholesale / retail arbitrage** | Buy branded stock from authorised sources and resell | Compliant, but brand permissions and thin margins |
| **Drop shipping where the supplier ships a branded parcel** | Customer orders on Amazon; supplier posts its own parcel | **Prohibited.** You must be the seller of record. Supplier names must be absent from parcels, packing slips, invoices and inserts. Violations can restrict fulfilment and the account. |

If a supplier fulfils orders on your behalf, you remain responsible for accuracy, shipping promises, returns and policy compliance (per SellerSprite's summary of Amazon's policy; read the policy itself in Seller Central).

## What you need in the UAE (as of July 2026, secondary sources)
- A **valid UAE trade licence** uploaded to Seller Central. Sellers who registered with ID only had until the end of 2025 to comply. [BusinessDubai]
- A licence activity covering online retail. Indicative costs: free zone e-commerce from about AED 5,750; mainland Dubai from about AED 15,000. The firm notes the home-based DED Trader licence is a poor fit for FBA. These are one vendor's numbers.
- An **importer code** with the emirate's customs authority. Amazon is not the importer of record, and without a code goods cannot clear. [Epic Sourcing; BusinessDubai]
- **Brand registration:** Amazon Brand Registry is free but needs a trademark. (Process to be verified.)

## Fees on Amazon.ae (snapshot July 2026)
| Item | Level | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Referral fee | 15% home, kitchen, apparel, shoes, luggage, books; 10% most other categories; beauty/health/baby/pet 8% to AED 50 and 15% above; electronics 7%; computers and major appliances 6%; phones 5% | A percentage of total price or a minimum, whichever is greater (Amazon) |
| FBA fulfilment | From AED 5.5 (envelope) to about AED 7.2–19.5 (standard up to 12 kg); AED 10.5–39.5 (oversize up to 30 kg); about AED 2 extra per unit above AED 25 | Per unit, by weight and size; includes picking, packing, shipping, customer service and returns |
| Storage | About AED 2 per cubic foot per month | Long-term storage rate not published on the page reviewed |
| Subscription | Zero for a limited time on Amazon's page; widely reported at AED 184 a month otherwise | Promotional: expect it to change |
| Advertising | Pay-per-click auction | You set the maximum bid |

## Import and tax (UAE)
- **Customs duty:** 5% of CIF value for most goods (GCC common tariff). Some categories differ.
- **Import VAT:** 5% on CIF plus duty, recoverable if you are VAT-registered, giving an effective cash cost of about 10.25% of landed value at the border.
- **VAT registration:** mandatory above AED 375,000 of taxable supplies in 12 months; voluntary from AED 187,500. The seller, not Amazon, accounts for VAT on sales.
- **Corporate tax:** 9% on taxable income above AED 375,000. A qualifying free zone 0% rate applies only to qualifying income; one firm reads Ministerial Decision 229 of 2025 as excluding B2C sales to consumers, which would catch Amazon sales. Small Business Relief may apply below AED 3 million revenue for periods ending on or before 31 Dec 2029. **This is the single most important item for an adviser to confirm**: see [[UAE Corporate Tax Essentials]] and [[UAE Holding Structures]].

## Economics: a worked example (illustrative, not a quote)
A small, light home product, VAT-registered, Amazon.ae:

| Per unit | AED |
|---|---|
| Shelf price (VAT-inclusive) | 99.00 |
| Net revenue after 5% VAT | 94.29 |
| Factory price (about USD 4.1) | 15.00 |
| Freight to FBA | 3.00 |
| Inspection | 0.50 |
| Customs duty 5% on CIF (15 + 3) | 0.90 |
| **Landed cost** | **19.40** |
| Referral fee 15% of 99 | 14.85 |
| FBA fee (assumed) | 9.00 |
| Storage and returns allowance | 1.14 |
| **Contribution before ads** | **49.90** |
| Advertising at 15% of price | 14.85 |
| **Net profit per unit** | **35.05** (37% of net revenue, 181% return on stock) |

This is a best-case teaching example. Real products often land closer to 10 to 20% margin after ads, returns and price competition. The Commerce Lab calculator reruns this with your own quotes; the rule of thumb in the app is **revenue at least 3x landed cost and 25% net margin after ads**.

## Cash is the real constraint
Stock is paid for 2 to 4 months before it sells. Illustrative simulation using the example product above: eight launches, one every two months; AED 12,000 first order and AED 3,000 launch advertising per launch; a 30% hit rate; winners reach 200 units a month over four months; two-month order-to-shelf lead time; 40% of a failed first order recovered; AED 1,500 a month overhead. Result: **peak cash need about AED 40,000, cumulative profit turning positive around month 16, and about AED 15,000 a month of profit by month 24**. At a 15% hit rate the same programme needs about AED 62,000 of peak cash and only just breaks even around month 24. Failed launches cost the first order and launch advertising, which is why the model assumes most launches fail. Rerun it with your own numbers in the Cash forecast tab.

## What can really be automated
| Function | Realistic route |
|---|---|
| Product scouting | Claude's twice-weekly scout, then 15 minutes of your review |
| Supplier search and negotiation | Sourcing agent or service; you approve |
| Quality control | Third-party inspection before each shipment |
| Freight and customs | Door-to-FBA forwarder with a broker |
| Listings and images | Freelancer or agency with AI drafting |
| Advertising | Agency or Amazon ad rules with caps |
| Pricing, inventory alerts | Amazon automated pricing and alerts |
| Fulfilment, returns, customer service | FBA |
| Bookkeeping and VAT | Accountant using the ledger |
| Strategy and decisions | You: about 30 minutes a week |

The app's Automation tab models this at about 4 hours a week once running (model assumption: delegated work still costs about 15% of your time to review, automated about 5%). Setup and each launch take far longer.

## Where it breaks
- **Product selection:** hype products with weak margins, heavy competition or seasonality.
- **Compliance and IP:** branded or patented designs, gated categories, products needing safety certification (batteries, electrical, children's, cosmetics, food contact). Check UAE and GCC conformity rules by product type.
- **Reviews:** only Amazon-approved programmes; incentivised or fake reviews end accounts.
- **Cash:** stock-outs hurt rank; overstock burns cash.
- **Fees drift:** Amazon changes fees; margins shrink.
- **Single-platform risk:** one suspension stops the income. Build your own store and Noon alongside.

## Key takeaways
- Be the seller of record; private label with FBA is the compliant, low-input route.
- Kill products on maths: 3x price-to-cost and 25% net margin after ads, no red flags.
- Budget for months of tied-up cash and for most launches to fail.
- Get a UAE trade licence, an importer code, and an adviser's view on VAT and corporate tax before ordering.
- Automate by delegation and Amazon's own tools; stay in charge of product choice and the weekly review.
