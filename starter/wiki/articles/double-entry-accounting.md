# Double-Entry Accounting

> **Bottom line:** Every transaction touches at least two accounts with equal debits and credits. That keeps the equation **Assets = Liabilities + Equity** true at all times and produces the three statements that tell you whether a business is healthy: profit and loss, balance sheet and cash flow.

## The rules
| Account type | Increases with | Normal balance |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | Debit | Debit |
| Expense | Debit | Debit |
| Liability | Credit | Credit |
| Equity | Credit | Credit |
| Income | Credit | Credit |

## Common entries
| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Owner invests AED 50,000 | 1100 Bank | 3000 Owner's capital |
| Pay AED 12,000 trade licence | 6600 Licences & fees | 1100 Bank |
| Sell golf bags for AED 8,500 cash | 1100 Bank | 4000 Sales revenue |
| Buy AED 3,000 leather on credit | 1300 Inventory | 2000 Accounts payable |
| Receive AED 9,000 rent | 1100 Bank | 4100 Rental income |

## The three statements
- **Profit & loss** — income minus expenses over a period. Shows whether you make money.
- **Balance sheet** — assets, liabilities and equity at a moment. Shows what you own and owe.
- **Cash flow** — cash in and out. Shows whether you can pay the bills; profitable businesses still die of cash shortages.

## Controls that build trust
- Reconcile every bank account monthly against the ledger.
- Keep one set of books per legal entity — never mix personal and business.
- Attach a reference (invoice or receipt number) to every entry.
- Review the trial balance: total debits must equal total credits.

## Key takeaways
- Two sides to every transaction; the books always balance.
- One ledger per entity, reconciled monthly.
- Profit is not cash; watch both.
