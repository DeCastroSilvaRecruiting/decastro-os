# Venture Dossier: Wednesday Circuit

> **Bottom line:** The market backdrop is favourable — 13 Dubai courses running "near capacity" in peak season and a fast-growing business base (292,486 active Chamber members at end-2025, +13%). Two facts found on 14 Nov 2026 reshape the first steps: (1) **one operator, Viya / Dubai Golf, runs five of Dubai's premier 18-hole courses** (plus three in Abu Dhabi), so tee access is essentially one negotiation; and (2) Dubai law requires a **Dubai Sports Council (DSC) permit for any organised sports event**, commercial or not, with an **AED 20,000 fine** for running one without it and no general exemption for private events. **First tests: a written Viya tee-block quote, a written DSC answer on a recurring members' round, and 10 paid founding-member commitments.**

## Context
Decision informed: whether to move Wednesday Circuit from Validate to Plan, and what to test first. Venture: C-suite golf networking and corporate golf events in Dubai/UAE, Wednesday-morning tee times, later regional. See [[Venture Validation Framework]].

This dossier was first written on 8 Nov 2026 and upgraded on 14 Nov 2026 (idle step): new sections on the operator landscape and Dubai sports-event law, refreshed green fees and membership prices, and a three-case worked example.

## Market and capacity (as of Nov 2026)
- **Courses:** 26 in the UAE — 13 in Dubai, 9 in Abu Dhabi (all courses, not only 18-hole championship courses); new capacity coming at Discovery Dunes, an Emaar course in Dubai South and a third 18-hole course at Jumeirah Golf Estates [1].
- **Utilisation:** Dubai Golf's CEO says courses run "at near capacity, especially during peak season" [1]. Membership is sold out across the board: Emirates Golf Club (all categories) [3], Dubai Creek corporate membership [16] and the Viya Premium Membership [17] all say they are at capacity with a waitlist (as of Nov 2026).
- **Golfers:** "22,000-plus resident golfers" (unsourced estimate) [10]. No current official rounds-per-season data found; the last credible study (Deloitte, 2018) counted ~410,000 rounds/yr across 9 Dubai clubs — stale.
- **Business base:** Dubai Chamber active members 292,486 at end-2025 (+13.2%) [7]; Dubai won 504 business-event bids in 2025 (+15%) and hosted 481 events [6].
- **Season:** usable peak Oct–Apr (~7 months); summer rates roughly half of peak [12]. One T3 guide narrows true high season to roughly Dec–Mar, with Oct, Nov and Apr "a little cheaper" [22].

## Who controls the tee sheet (new, Nov 2026)
- **Viya / Dubai Golf** (both part of wasl Hospitality & Leisure [20]) operates Emirates Golf Club (Majlis and Faldo), Jumeirah Golf Estates (Earth and Fire) and Dubai Creek Golf & Yacht Club in Dubai, and manages Aldar's Yas Links, Saadiyat Beach and Yas Acres in Abu Dhabi [19][20]. Its Golfcation Pass (Apr 2026) bundles these courses with hotel nights at AED 1,999 (one round + one night) [19].
- **Booking channel:** member tee times are booked exclusively through the Viya app, with an advance window of **11 days at Dubai Creek and 7 days at Emirates GC and JGE** (as of Nov 2026) [16]. A recurring weekly block is therefore not something a member can self-book; it has to be negotiated as an event or commercial arrangement.
- **Corporate golf days:** both Dubai Creek and JGE sell corporate golf days direct (day-only or with an evening gala), priced on request [2][18].
- **Independents:** Dubai Hills Golf Club (Emaar), Trump International, The Els Club, Montgomerie, Arabian Ranches/Al Badia — the fallback if Viya will not commit a block.

Judgement: concentration cuts both ways. One good relationship with Viya's events team could unlock five premier courses on rotation (a selling point: "a different championship course each month") and a regional Abu Dhabi leg. One refusal closes most of the premier inventory. Viya is also a potential **competitor**: it already sells corporate golf days, memberships and passes, and could copy a successful format.

## Licensing and permits (to confirm with DSC, DET and an adviser)
**Dubai Sports Council permit — new finding (T1, Executive Council Resolution No. 1 of 2020) [14]:**
- A "sports event" is any sports activity or function organised in Dubai **for commercial or non-commercial purposes** (Art. 1). No one may organise one **without first obtaining a DSC permit** (Art. 5(a)).
- **Venues** may not let anyone hold an event without a DSC permit (Art. 5(b)), so clubs may ask to see it.
- To get a permit the organiser must **hold a licence for holding events** in Dubai, plus venue approval, a safety plan, qualified staff, third-party insurance and sponsor disclosure (Art. 7(a)). DSC's Secretary General can waive any of these except the naming rules (Art. 7(b)).
- Obligations include ambulance/safety cover, **DSC approval before advertising**, displaying the DSC logo and a post-event report (Art. 16).
- **Exemption:** the resolution does not apply to sports activities that companies or government bodies organise **for their own employees** (Art. 2(c)). There is no general exemption for private or invite-only events.
- **Fines (AED, schedule):** organising without a permit 20,000; venue hosting an unpermitted event 10,000; advertising without approval 5,000; failing on safety/ambulance 10,000; misuse of "Dubai" in the name 25,000. Repeat offences within a year double, up to 150,000 (Art. 19).
- **Naming:** an event may not use "Dubai" in its name without prior DSC approval, which is reserved for licensed entities whose event has "significant positive impact" on Dubai (DSC Decision No. 1 of 2021) [15]. "Wednesday Circuit" is fine; avoid names like "Dubai CEO Golf Series".
- Not found: the DSC permit fee and processing time (DSC service pages were not reachable in this run).

**DET (commercial licence and event permit):**
- DET event permit covers corporate and sport events, applied via DET ePermits [5]; fees and lead times still not confirmed from the official page (the page could not be re-read on 14 Nov 2026).
- Commercial guide (T3) [13]: mainland DET licence (event organising) for mainland venues; venue NOC first; permit ~3–6 working days, apply ≥15 days before promotion; mainland licence ~AED 12–20k. Event management is **not** a free zone qualifying activity, so expect 9% CT above AED 375k — see [[UAE Corporate Tax Essentials]].

Judgement: a weekly paid round organised by Wednesday Circuit for outside executives fits the Art. 1 definition, so plan for a DSC permit (ideally a single season-long permit covering the recurring series, since permits run "for the period stated in them", Art. 9(b)). The employee exemption suggests one lower-friction product: **white-label golf days run on behalf of a corporate client for its own staff** — but whether a third-party organiser keeps the exemption must be confirmed with DSC. This is a legal question for a licensed adviser, not a conclusion.

## Worked example — one event (illustrative AED, assumptions labelled)
24 executives, premier course, peak season. Price charged **2,500/head + VAT** (assumption to test). Fixed per head: lunch 80 + dinner 185 [2]; photographer and pin flags 3,675 per event [2] = 153/head. Three green-fee cases from the conflicting sources:

| Case | Green fee/head | Cost/head | Contribution/head | Per event (×24) | 20-event season |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low (JGE Earth range, [22]) | 700 | 1,118 | 1,382 | ≈ 33,200 | ≈ 664,000 |
| Mid (base case) | 1,000 | 1,418 | 1,082 | ≈ 26,000 | ≈ 519,000 |
| High (CaddieHQ range, [12]) | 1,400 | 1,818 | 682 | ≈ 16,400 | ≈ 327,000 |

- Before staff, marketing, insurance, DSC/DET permit fees (unknown) and the licence (~AED 12–20k, T3).
- **Compliance maths:** one unpermitted event risks an AED 20,000 fine for us and 10,000 for the venue — close to a whole event's contribution in the high case. Permits are cheap insurance; skipping them is not a saving.
- **Price sensitivity:** at 1,800/head the mid case falls to ~9,200/event. One title sponsor at AED 100k/season still covers ~4 mid-case events' margin — sponsorship matters as much as fees.
- **Tax:** the mid-case season, less a ~16k licence and any overheads, sits above the AED 375k CT threshold, so budget 9% on the excess.

## Riskiest assumptions (judgement) — test in this order
1. **Tee access:** a recurring peak-season Wednesday block at a premier course at a known price. *Test:* one written proposal to Viya's events team (rotation across EGC, JGE, Dubai Creek) and one to Dubai Hills as the independent fallback.
2. **Permit route:** a recurring members' series can run under one season permit at an acceptable cost and lead time. *Test:* written enquiry to DSC (and DET) describing the format exactly; ask whether a white-label client day qualifies for the employee exemption.
3. **Willingness to pay** a premium over a club golf day and over free sponsor events. *Test:* 10 founding members paying a deposit.
4. **Weekday availability** of C-suite for ~5 hours recurring. *Test:* pilot 3 consecutive Wednesdays, measure attendance.
5. **Sponsor appetite** (banks, wealth managers, auto). *Test:* 5 sponsor conversations with a one-page deck.
6. **Summer gap:** simulator/indoor formats (e.g. Topgolf — also a Viya-linked brand [19][20] — Five Iron) — untested. Update 17 Nov 2026: Five Iron Golf opened a second UAE site in Business Bay (announced 26 May 2026; five simulators and a private room for corporate gatherings, league-friendly booking) and planned a 12-simulator Yas Bay Abu Dhabi site for mid-Aug 2026 with leagues and corporate events (First Call Golf press releases, T2). Venues exist and court groups, but no league or group rates were found, so pricing is still untested.

## Key takeaways
- Scarce peak tee times are both the moat and the main risk — and one operator (Viya) holds most of the premier ones.
- Every organised paid round likely needs a DSC permit and a licensed organiser; running without one risks AED 20k per event.
- Incumbents are annual, SME-focused or open societies; a curated weekly C-suite format is the gap.
- Unit economics hold at ~AED 2,500/head in all three green-fee cases; the high case plus no sponsor gets thin.
- Only ~7 good months a year; Dec–Mar is the true peak.

## What this means for the empire
- Stay in **Validate**. Spend under AED 20k: the Viya and Dubai Hills proposals, the DSC/DET enquiry, a 3-event pilot with a hand-picked list (run as a permitted event or as a white-label client day once the exemption is confirmed), founding-member deposits.
- **Do not take deposits or advertise publicly before the licence and permit route is clear** — advertising without DSC approval is itself a fine (AED 5,000).
- Keep "Dubai" out of the brand and event names.
- Cross-sell with [[Venture Dossier: DCS Golf]] — event gifts and a captive luxury golf audience.
- Use a mainland operating company under the holdco described in [[UAE Holding Structures]].
- **E-invoicing (added 15 Nov 2026, weekly review):** Wednesday Circuit bills corporates and sponsors, so its sales are B2B and fall inside the UAE e-invoicing system. For businesses under AED 50m revenue an accredited service provider must be appointed by **31 Mar 2027** and e-invoicing goes live **1 Jul 2027**; Cabinet Decision 173/2026 sets penalties of AED 5,000 a month, capped at AED 50,000 (as of Nov 2026) — see [[UAE Corporate Tax Essentials]]. Pick invoicing software that is (or will be) connected to an accredited provider from day one rather than migrating mid-season. Confirm scope and dates with a licensed tax adviser.
