# Venture Validation Framework

> **Bottom line:** Most ventures fail because nobody wanted what was built at a price that made money — not because they were built badly. Before spending real capital, find the assumption most likely to kill the idea and test it as cheaply as possible. Release money in stages, each unlocked by evidence.

## The stages in De Castro OS
| Stage | Goal | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | Capture and score | Thesis written; score ≥ your bar |
| Validate | Prove demand and price | Riskiest assumptions validated with real buyers |
| Plan | Prove the model | Unit economics work on paper with real quotes |
| Build | Minimum version | First paying customers |
| Launch | Repeatable sales | Predictable acquisition at known cost |
| Scale | Grow the engine | LTV:CAC healthy, cash-flow positive or funded |

## Assumption mapping
List everything that must be true, then rank by **risk if wrong × uncertainty**. Typical categories:
- **Desirability** — do they have the problem, and will they pay?
- **Viability** — price, margin, acquisition cost, lifetime value
- **Feasibility** — can we make and deliver it; suppliers; skills
- **Permission** — licences, regulation, land, permits

## Cheap tests, in order of cost
1. Twenty problem interviews — ask about past behaviour, not hypothetical futures (*The Mom Test*).
2. A landing page or WhatsApp catalogue with a price and a "reserve" button.
3. Pre-sales or paid pilots — the strongest evidence is money.
4. A concierge or manual version before automating anything.

## Unit economics checks
- Contribution margin per unit = price − variable cost
- LTV = margin × purchases per period × customer lifetime
- **LTV : CAC ≥ 3** and **CAC payback under 12 months** are widely used benchmarks for a healthy model.

## Key takeaways
- Test the riskiest assumption first.
- Money from customers is the only proof of demand.
- Stage capital behind evidence.
- Scale only after unit economics work.

## What this means for the empire
Each venture in the Venture Engine has an assumptions table and a "Test next" prompt. For example, Wednesday Circuit's riskiest assumption is likely whether companies will pay a premium per head for executive golf events; DCS Golf's is likely whether buyers will pay a luxury price for a new hand-made brand; the Al Ain farm's are permits, land and visitor demand. Diligence (Nov 2026) is in the three venture dossiers: tee-time access for Wednesday Circuit, price acceptance and licence eligibility for DCS Golf, and permits/stock supply for the Al Ain farm are the first tests.
