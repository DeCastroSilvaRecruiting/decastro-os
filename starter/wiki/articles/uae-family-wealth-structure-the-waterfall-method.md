# UAE Family Wealth Structure: The Waterfall Method

> **Bottom line:** The UAE version of the Waterfall Method is a foundation (ADGM or DIFC) at the top, a holding company under it that owns each business and property in its own company, and permanent life policies owned by the foundation whose payouts land back in it and fund the next generation's cover and a family bank. The legal structure is cheap; the insurance is the big, long commitment, so register a will first, build the foundation and holding company next, and size policies only once the businesses produce cash.

## Context

The Waterfall Method, as marketed (mostly in the US), uses permanent life insurance owned by a family entity so that each payout funds the next generation's policies. Its US appeal is largely estate-tax driven. The UAE has no inheritance tax, so for a Dubai resident the value is **succession, asset protection and liquidity**, not tax. This article adapts the method to UAE structures and is the research behind the **Dynasty** module in De Castro OS. Full blueprint: the owner's "De Castro Family Wealth Structure — Waterfall Method Blueprint" doc (17 Nov 2026).

## The structure

- **Foundation at the apex, not a trust.** A UAE foundation is its own legal entity with no shares, so assets inside it do not pass through probate on death. ADGM is the cheaper and more private option (council details not on the public register); DIFC has the deeper family-office ecosystem but puts council details on the public register.
- **Holding company under the foundation.** An ADGM SPV or DIFC Prescribed Company, 100% owned by the foundation. Each business (DCS Golf, Wednesday Circuit, Al Ain Ostrich Farm, Global Trend Store) and each property sits in its own company beneath it, so a failure or lawsuit in one cannot reach the others. See [[UAE Holding Structures]] for the holdco options and the property-holding caveats.
- **Policies owned by the foundation.** Policy owner: the foundation. Beneficiary: the foundation. Life insured: the family member. Premiums are paid by the foundation, funded by dividends from the holding company. The video version of the method names the holding company as beneficiary; holding the policies at foundation level keeps payouts out of a trading group and lets a tax-transparent foundation receive them. Which is better is an adviser question.
- **Recycling rule.** The charter directs part of each payout to new policies on the next generation and the rest to the family bank.
- **Registered will.** Without a registered DIFC or Abu Dhabi will, UAE default inheritance rules apply to UAE assets. The will pours anything left outside the structure into the foundation and names guardians.

## Setup in six phases

| Phase | What | Indicative cost (as of Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Will | Register through the DIFC Wills Service (common law) or the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department; executor, guardians, foundation as residuary beneficiary | DIFC about AED 10,000; ADJD about AED 950 |
| 2. Design the foundation | Purpose, founder's reserved powers, council (you plus one or two trusted people or a professional), optional guardian, beneficiaries defined by class, charter and by-laws including the waterfall and family bank rules | Professional fees, quoted per case |
| 3. Register the foundation | Via a corporate service provider, 4 to 8 weeks including drafting; then apply to the Federal Tax Authority for Qualifying Family Foundation (tax-transparent) status | ADGM registry USD 1,000 then USD 500 a year; DIFC USD 350 then USD 650 a year; ADGM registered office from about USD 6,000 a year |
| 4. Holding company | ADGM SPV or DIFC Prescribed Company owned by the foundation; one company per business or property; Corporate Tax registration for each | ADGM SPV from about AED 25,000 |
| 5. Life policies | Independent fee-based broker; compare whole life, universal life and term at the same cover with year-by-year surrender values; PPLI only at private-bank scale | Quote-based: age, health, cover, policy type |
| 6. Governance | Family constitution, letter of wishes, annual family meeting, yearly review and after every birth, marriage, move or business sale | Mostly time |

Plan on roughly **USD 15,000 to 30,000 in year one** for the will, foundation and one holding company, mostly professional fees. A full DIFC single-family-office stack (foundation plus two Prescribed Companies) runs about USD 50,000 to 80,000 in year one and from USD 25,000 a year after, aimed at families with about USD 10 million investable. Fee figures come mostly from service providers and differ slightly between sources: confirm against the registries' own fee schedules (the ADGM and DIFC schedules are cited in [[UAE Holding Structures]]).

## Complementary methods

| Method | What it adds | Fit now |
|---|---|---|
| Ring-fenced companies | One company per business or property | High, with the holding company |
| Key-person and shareholder cover | Cheap term policies owned by each business | High once a business has revenue or a partner |
| Family constitution and tiered distributions | Who sits on the council, how heirs qualify, the order money flows (education, housing, seed capital, discretionary) | High, with the charter |
| Family bank | The foundation lends to family ventures on written terms; repayments refill the pool | High once policies have cash value, usually year 5 or later |
| Term cover plus invested savings | Cheaper bridge while capital is scarce | Medium, now |
| Private Placement Life Insurance (PPLI) | Wraps an investment portfolio in a bespoke policy | Low until private-bank scale |
| UAE onshore trust | Reported to be available under a 2023 federal decree-law as an alternative to a free-zone foundation (not yet verified against the legislation) | Compare only |

**South Africa.** As a non-resident, South Africa charges estate duty only on South African-situated assets: 20% up to R30 million and 25% above, after a R3.5 million abatement (SARS, accessed Nov 2026). If someone is still treated as ordinarily resident, it can reach the worldwide estate. Confirm residency status formally, and hold or sell remaining South African assets deliberately.

## Risks

- **Premium strain.** Whole life is the most expensive cover and early surrender usually returns less than was paid. Never commit to premiums the holding company cannot pay from profit for 10+ years.
- **Sales bias.** The Waterfall and PPLI are heavily marketed. Use a fee-based adviser and demand a year-by-year surrender schedule. Unit-linked plans sold onshore must include a signed surrender-charge page and a cancellation window of at least 30 days.
- **Beneficiary conflicts.** A policy pays whoever the contract names, even if the will says otherwise. Keep the foundation as named beneficiary and review nominations after every life event.
- **Paper-only structures fail.** Banks, courts and tax authorities test whether a foundation is really run: council meetings, minutes, separate bank accounts, arm's-length loans.
- **Tax assumptions.** Confirm Corporate Tax treatment of payouts and Qualifying Family Foundation status before funding.
- **Cross-border reach.** Re-check if anyone in the family moves country.

## Key takeaways

- Foundation on top, holding company beneath, one company per venture or property, policies owned by and paying to the foundation.
- The legal structure is affordable (roughly USD 15,000 to 30,000 in year one); insurance is the expensive part.
- Will first; insurance last.
- In the UAE the benefit is succession, protection and liquidity, not inheritance tax.
- The structure only protects if it is run: minutes, reviews, separate accounts.

- ADGM or DIFC for the foundation, given privacy and ecosystem needs? (UAE structuring lawyer)

- Policies in the foundation or the holding company: what is the tax difference? (UAE tax adviser)
- Can the will pour UAE assets into the foundation, and which registry fits? (lawyer)
- Is the owner clearly non-resident for South African tax, and what is left in South Africa? (South African tax adviser)
