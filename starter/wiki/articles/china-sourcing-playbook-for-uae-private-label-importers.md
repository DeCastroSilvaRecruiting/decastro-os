# China Sourcing Playbook for UAE Private-Label Importers

> **Bottom line:** For a first order, buy through **Alibaba Trade Assurance** or from a **verified Global Sources supplier**. Check the business licence yourself, sign an NNN agreement and file your trademark in China **before** you share the design, and pay **30% deposit / 70% balance, released only after a passed third-party inspection** (named providers publish USD 268–298 a man-day, all-inclusive in China). Buy FOB or FCA, and import under **your own Dubai customs code**: Amazon is not the importer of record. **The big change in 2026 is freight.** Since March the Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed to container shipping, and it still was in early November. China–UAE sea cargo now lands mostly at Khor Fakkan or Fujairah and is trucked to Dubai. Plan 6–8 weeks door-to-FBA by sea and budget freight at two to four times pre-war levels until this clears.

*Confidence is medium. Platform rules, verification schemes, Dubai Customs rules and inspection prices come from the providers' own pages, but several of those pages carry no date and some prices are from 2023. Every freight figure comes from trade press or forwarders, and the Gulf shipping situation changes week to week. Amazon.ae's own FBA inbound rules are behind the Seller Central login and were not read on this run.*

## Context
The **Global Trend Store** venture plans to import private-label products from Chinese factories into Amazon.ae FBA. [[Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics]] set out the model and its unit economics. This article is the operating manual for the step that article assumed: finding, verifying, paying and shipping from a factory without losing the deposit, the design or the margin. It informs three decisions:
1. Which platform and protection to use for the first order.
2. Which payment terms and Incoterm to agree.
3. Whether the freight assumption in the venture's calculator (AED 3 a unit) still holds while the Gulf is disrupted. It does not, as shown below.

**DCS Golf** can reuse the same playbook if it sources components such as hardware, zips, canvas or leather trims from China.

## Where to find suppliers

| Platform | What it is | Buyer protection | Verification signal | Best use for us |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alibaba.com** | English-language B2B marketplace; mix of factories and traders | **Trade Assurance**: escrow-style. Funds are released after you confirm receipt, but only when the order is placed and paid through Alibaba.com [2] | "Verified Supplier": an on-site audit of 100+ items in 8 categories by **SGS or TÜV Rheinland**, with the report on the supplier's profile [3] | **Default for the first orders** |
| **1688.com** | Alibaba's domestic Chinese wholesale site; Chinese-language, priced in RMB | No Trade Assurance described for overseas buyers; payment usually in RMB via Alipay or a Chinese account [6] | Domestic ratings only | Price benchmarking; later re-orders through an agent who can read Chinese and consolidate freight [6] |
| **Global Sources** | Hong Kong-based B2B platform and trade shows; tends towards established exporters | Does **not** intervene in buyer–supplier commercial disputes [4] | "Verified Supplier" means business registration checked by D&B, Experian, TÜV SÜD and others. "Verified Manufacturer" means the licensed business scope allows manufacturing. An on-site "Supplier Capability Assessment" is optional and paid [4] | Electronics and gifts; meeting suppliers at shows; separating factories from traders |
| **Made-in-China.com** | Chinese B2B platform with a strong industrial and machinery range | Not established on this run | "Audit reports" produced by **SGS**, covering licences, certifications and production/QC systems [5] | Hardware and components (relevant to DCS Golf) |

*Judgement:* use Alibaba for the first order, because Trade Assurance is the only platform-level money protection we found. Use Global Sources' "Verified Manufacturer" flag as a cheap factory-versus-trader filter, and use 1688 only to see what the factory-gate price really is.

## Verifying a supplier (checklist)
Do all of this **before** paying anything:

1. **Get the business licence (营业执照) and look it up yourself** on China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (gsxt.gov.cn). Search the company's exact legal Chinese name or its unified social credit code. If nothing comes back, either the company does not exist or the name is wrong. The site is in Chinese but can be reached from outside China [7]. *Not re-tested on this run; the source is from 2022.*
2. **Match three names:** the name on the licence, the name on the Alibaba profile and the beneficiary name on the bank account. A mismatch is the most common sign of fraud (*Judgement*).
3. **Check the business scope** on the licence. If it does not include manufacturing (生产/制造), you are dealing with a trader, whatever they say. This is the same test Global Sources uses for "Verified Manufacturer" [4].
4. **Read the third-party report:** Alibaba Verified (SGS/TÜV) [3], Made-in-China audit (SGS) [5] or Global Sources capability assessment [4]. Check the date and the address. The audited address should be the factory you will inspect.
5. **Check for product certificates** your category needs in the UAE/GCC (for example electrical safety or food contact). Ask for test reports and verify the report number with the issuing lab (*Judgement*; UAE conformity rules are an open question in the parent article).
6. **Hold a video call from the production floor**, then book a **factory audit** (from USD 280 a man-day at QC Advisor [16]) before any order above about AED 20,000 (*Judgement* on the threshold).
7. **Keep all communication and payment on the platform**, or Trade Assurance protection is lost [2].

## Factory vs trading company

| | Factory | Trading company |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Lowest | Usually a mark-up (size not established from a neutral source) |
| MOQ | Higher; set by production runs | Often lower; they combine orders |
| Customisation and tooling | Direct control | Second-hand; tooling may sit at a factory you never see |
| Range | Narrow | Broad; one contact for several products |
| Licence scope | Includes manufacturing | Trading/import-export only [4] |

*Judgement:* a good trader is acceptable for a first, small, low-customisation order. For anything with your own mould, logo or pattern, you want the factory itself, or at least the factory's name, address and licence written into the contract.

## Payment terms and protection
- **Trade Assurance** is free to buyers. Suppliers pay to take part [10].
  - It covers product quality against the agreed specification and the shipment date [10].
  - Refund requests can be made before shipment and up to **60 days after delivery**, according to Alibaba's own guide [2]. One UAE-focused forwarder reports the window as "usually 30 days after the expected delivery date" [10]. **Treat 30 days as the safe deadline** and check the date shown on each order.
  - Each order shows a "Trade Assurance amount" that can be lower than what you paid [10]. Check it before you pay.
- **Alibaba's separate "Guaranteed" delivery product** pays only 10% of order value for late dispatch, capped at USD 100, and pays it in coupons. Its refund and Easy Return cover is limited to orders of USD 3,000 or less [1]. Do not mistake it for real protection on a stock order.
- **Not covered** [10]:
  - delays in UAE customs;
  - damage after handover to your own forwarder;
  - side deals agreed off the platform.
  Disputes over private-label goods are harder to win because "quality" is subjective, so write measurable specifications into the order.
- **30/70 T/T is the market norm:** 30% deposit at order and 70% before shipment. Harris Sliwoski warns that under these terms you pay in full before you have inspected anything. It recommends making the 70% due **only after a passed quality inspection**, inspecting at more than one production stage, and moving to milestone or after-inspection payment as trust builds. Suing is rarely worth it on orders under about USD 50,000–100,000 [8].
- *Judgement:* for the first order, use Trade Assurance with the 30/70 split, and write "balance payable on PASS of third-party PSI by [named provider] at AQL [x]" into the order terms.

## Samples
*Judgement and practitioner practice; no Tier 1 source applies.*
- Order samples from **three shortlisted suppliers** and expect to pay for samples and courier. Ask for the sample cost to be credited against the first order.
- Then order a **pre-production ("golden") sample** from the chosen supplier, with your logo, packaging and FNSKU label. Sign and photograph it, keep one, and give the inspector a copy. It becomes the reference for any dispute.
- Test against what Amazon customers will complain about: dimensions, materials, smell, stitching, function and packaging drop.

## MOQ negotiation
*Judgement; no neutral data on typical discounts was found.*
- Listed MOQs are often an opening price, not a rule. Factories set them to cover set-up, materials bought in bulk and production-line changeover.
- Ways to get a lower MOQ:
  - accept a stock colour or material;
  - pay a higher unit price for the first run;
  - pay a one-off set-up fee;
  - split the order across colours;
  - commit in writing to a re-order volume.
- For FBA, the first order should be **sized to the test, not to the price break**. Per the parent article, most launches fail and 60% of a failed first order is lost. Paying AED 1 a unit more on 500 units is cheaper than writing off 2,000.

## Incoterms (ICC Incoterms 2020)

| Rule | Seller's job ends | What it means for a UAE FBA importer |
|---|---|---|
| **EXW** Ex Works | Goods made available at the factory. Buyer handles export clearance, loading and everything after [11] | Avoid for China. You become responsible for Chinese export clearance. ICC calls EXW best suited to domestic trade and suggests **FCA** when the buyer may struggle with export clearance [11]. |
| **FCA** Free Carrier (any mode) [12] | Goods handed, export-cleared, to your forwarder | Best for **air, LCL and consolidations**. Your forwarder controls the main leg. |
| **FOB** Free on Board (sea only) [12] | Goods loaded on the vessel at the Chinese port | **Recommended default for sea.** Your forwarder books the main leg, insurance and the Dubai side. It is the price most suppliers quote. |
| **CIF** Cost, Insurance & Freight (sea only) [12] | Supplier pays freight and insurance to the UAE port; risk passes at loading | Looks convenient, but the supplier picks the cheapest carrier and the insurance minimum, and destination charges come back to you. In 2026 the supplier may also be quoting to a port the ship can no longer reach (*Judgement*). |
| **DDP** Delivered Duty Paid | Seller clears import and pays duty and VAT at destination [11] | ICC warns that some countries do not let foreign sellers complete import formalities and suggests DAP instead [11]. In the UAE a commercial import needs a licensed importer's customs code [20][21]. A "DDP door-to-FBA" offer from a Chinese forwarder therefore means **someone else's code is on your goods**. You lose control of the declared value and may be unable to recover the import VAT (*Judgement*; ask the forwarder whose code and TRN they will use). |

## Tooling and IP
- **China is first-to-file.** The first applicant generally gets the mark, and use outside China earns no priority. Anyone who sees your brand can file it first: the factory, an agent, the packaging printer or the forwarder. Harris Sliwoski cites a client who paid six figures to recover a squatted mark after its shipment was detained [9].
  - **File in China before you send artwork.** File in the name of the entity that will own the brand, and in the right subclasses [9].
- **CNIPA official fees** (schedule in force since 1 July 2019) [18]:
  - **CNY 270 per class online**, covering up to 10 items, plus CNY 27 per extra item;
  - registration typically takes about 12–18 months if uncontested;
  - a new Trademark Law takes effect on **1 January 2027**. It tightens the rules on bad-faith filings and shortens oppositions to 2 months [18].
  - **Madrid route:** China has been a member since 1995, but a Madrid designation depends on your home mark for 5 years. A direct Chinese filing avoids that risk [18].
- **UAE trademark:** the Ministry of Economy application fee is reported as **AED 750 per class**. A 50% reduction applies to registered SMEs, and a Madrid-conversion service has been added under Cabinet Resolution 102 of 2025 [19]. *This is a law firm's summary; confirm on moec.gov.ae.* Amazon Brand Registry needs a trademark (see parent article).
- **NNN agreement** (non-disclosure, non-use, non-circumvention). Sign it **before** you share specifications or designs. It binds only the parties who sign it, so it complements a trademark but does not replace one; most projects need both [9].
- **Tooling** (*Judgement*; not covered by the sources read): moulds you pay for should be stated in the contract as your property. The contract should:
  - give the tooling's location, an ID marking and photos;
  - ban its use for anyone else;
  - give you the right to move it.
  Write it in Chinese and English and make it enforceable in China. **A China-qualified lawyer should draft the NNN and manufacturing contract.**

## Freight to Amazon.ae FBA

### The 2026 situation (read this first)
- **The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since 28 February 2026.** Middle East container imports fell 64% year-on-year in March [28].
  - A US–Iran memorandum in mid-June raised hopes of reopening [30], but closure conditions returned in July [27].
  - On 26 October, Iran again declared the strait closed. The International Maritime Organization said it "cannot guarantee safe transit", and the US blockade of Iranian ports continues [36].
  - Lloyd's List counted 116 transits by large non-Iranian cargo ships in the week of 19–25 October, almost all tankers and gas carriers [35].
- **Jebel Ali:** handled about 374,000 TEU in Q2 2026, about 90% below a year earlier (forwarder citing port data). DP World says it could restore full capacity within 48 hours of normal shipping resuming [34].
- **How China–UAE boxes arrive now:** discharged **outside Hormuz** at Khor Fakkan or Fujairah (UAE) or Sohar (Oman), then trucked to Dubai [31][32].
  - Ships calling at Khor Fakkan faced delays of more than 10 days in March [31].
  - Khor Fakkan's weekly throughput rose from about 8,000 to 65,000 TEU, and capacity is being expanded [33].
  - Truck queues at the east-coast ports reach up to 12 hours, and overland legs cost several times more per tonne-km [33][34].
  - Dubai runs a customs "green corridor" for cargo trucked from Oman through Hatta [32].
- **Cost:** Xeneta reported China–Khor Fakkan container rates **up 300%** since the war began [32]. Carriers added conflict surcharges, for example CMA CGM and Hapag-Lloyd at **USD 3,000 per FEU** (2 March 2026) and MSC at **USD 800 per container** [30]. Air capacity into the Gulf was cut and fuel and war surcharges were added [29][30].

*Judgement:* while Hormuz stays closed, plan **6–8 weeks** from goods-ready to FBA check-in for sea LCL, and about 2 weeks for air. Ask every forwarder to name the discharge port and whether trucking from Khor Fakkan or Fujairah is in the price. Get quotes valid in writing for the shipment date, because surcharges change mid-month [27]. The FreightAmigo rate table (April 2026) does not appear to reflect east-coast rerouting. Use it as a floor, not a budget.

### FBA inbound packaging
- Amazon's US guidance (7 July 2026) [25] requires:
  - a scannable **FNSKU** label on every unit, not photocopied;
  - a **suffocation warning** on poly bags with an opening over 5 inches;
  - "sold as set" marking for bundles.
- Amazon.ae's own carton size and weight limits, box-content labelling and appointment rules are in Seller Central behind the login and **were not read on this run**. Get them from Seller Central before the factory packs.
- *Judgement:* have the factory apply FNSKU labels and the inspector check them. Relabelling in Dubai costs more than doing it in China.

- Clearance and port AED 900, an estimate from Epic Sourcing [24].
- Invoice attestation AED 150 [23].
- Trucking and prep to FBA AED 1,000 (*Judgement*).
- Insurance AED 75 (*Judgement*).

## Customs clearance in Dubai
- **Customs client code.** Every person or company dealing with Dubai Customs must register for a client code. You need a valid licence and the sponsor's passport copy. The code lasts as long as the licence and is suspended if not renewed within 60 days of expiry [20]. Licence holders selling locally obtain a **Customs Business Code**; registration is online through dubaitrade.ae [21].
- **Brokers.** A customs broker is a licensed company that prepares, signs and submits declarations and completes clearance on the owner's behalf [20].
  - Each broker holds an **AED 50,000 bank guarantee** and is responsible for the accuracy of what it declares [20].
  - Brokers need a Dubai Department of Economy Clearing & Forwarding licence and your written authorisation [21].
  - The goods owner may instead authorise its own representative [20].
  - *Judgement:* in practice your forwarder's in-house broker files under your code. Insist on seeing the declaration before it is submitted.
- **Documents** [20]:
  - customs declaration;
  - original commercial invoice, attested;
  - bill of lading or air waybill;
  - a detailed packing list for containerised or palletised goods;
  - on request: proof of payment, contract or pro forma invoice, and an Arabic description of foreign-language invoices.
  - A certificate of origin is not a general requirement in the policies reviewed. Ask your broker by product.
- **Invoice attestation.** Import invoices of **AED 10,000 or more** must be attested electronically by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs within **14 days of entry**. The fee is **AED 150**, with an **AED 500 fine** for missing the deadline (Cabinet Resolution 38 of 2022, as reported in 2023) [23].
- **Duty:** **5% of CIF value** for general goods (Dubai Customs FAQ) [21].
- **Import VAT: 5%.**
  - VAT-registered importers account for it **in the VAT return**, with no cash paid at the border [22].
  - Unregistered importers pay through an FTA-registered clearing company before release [22].
  - *Ask the adviser:* how to link the TRN to the customs code so the deferral works; the FTA guidance found dates from 2017–18.
- **Timing:** with correct documents and HS codes, clearance is often 24–48 hours (forwarder, June 2026) [24]. Rerouted east-coast cargo may also clear in Oman or move under bond [34].

## Key takeaways
- Verify the licence yourself on gsxt.gov.cn and match the licence, profile and bank-account names. Platform badges help but are not a check.
- Pay 30/70 through Trade Assurance, with the 70% tied in writing to a passed inspection (USD 268–299 a man-day from named providers). Open any dispute within 30 days.
- File the trademark in China and sign an NNN agreement before sharing designs. China is first-to-file and a new law arrives on 1 Jan 2027.
- Buy FOB (sea) or FCA (air), clear under your own customs code, and avoid "DDP" offers that use someone else's code.
- The Hormuz closure roughly doubles small-order sea freight and adds weeks. Budget about AED 5 a unit by sea, not 3, and launch by sea, not air.
