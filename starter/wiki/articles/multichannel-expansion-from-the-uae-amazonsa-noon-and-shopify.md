# Multichannel Expansion from the UAE: Amazon.sa, Noon and Shopify

> **Bottom line:** For Global Trend Store, the second channel should be **Noon UAE on FBN**. For a light AED 69 bundle its fees match Amazon.ae almost to the dirham (Sep 2026, [1]). Once the store is live on Noon UAE, Noon's **UAE-to-KSA "global store" lane** switches on automatically. Saudi buyers can then order with **no Saudi entity, no Saudi VAT registration and no stock in Saudi Arabia**, because Noon collects and pays the KSA VAT ([2][3], Apr–Nov 2026). **Amazon.sa with its own FBA stock is a different, heavier project.** It needs a Saudi importer, SABER certificates per product and shipment, and KSA VAT registration from the first riyal [4][10]. Leave it until Saudi demand is proven. **Shopify** is the wrong main channel for a low-ticket trend store, because customer acquisition eats the margin. It is the right home for **DCS Golf**.

*Confidence is medium. The Noon fee card and the Noon UAE-to-KSA rules come from Noon's own help centre (T1), and the KSA VAT position comes from ZATCA's guideline (T1). Several points remain unverified: an Amazon UAE-to-KSA "Remote Fulfillment" programme (only T3 sources describe it), Amazon.sa's entity rules for non-Saudi sellers, Shopify's UAE processing rates and plan limits (the Shopify pricing page and Stripe UAE pricing were not reachable this run), and cross-border courier rates. All of these are marked below.*

## Context
This informs **when and how Global Trend Store adds channels beyond Amazon.ae** (see [[Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics]]), and where DCS Golf should sell online. The question behind it: does a second marketplace or an own store add profit and resilience, or does it just split stock, cash and attention?

Saudi Arabia is the prize. Amazon cites forecasts of **19.3 million e-commerce users in KSA versus 5.7 million in the UAE by 2029** (Statista via Amazon, page © 2026 [6]; an estimate). Saudi VAT is 15%, against 5% in the UAE [12].

## Channel by channel

### 1. Noon UAE (Fulfilled by noon)
- **Fees (effective 10 Sep 2026, ex VAT) [1]:** referral is a percentage of price with an AED 1 minimum. Automotive "others" is **12%**. Pets: 9% for most items, 14% for toys, and pet furniture 8% up to AED 50 and 15% above. Home: 15% for most lines, and cleaning 7.5% up to AED 50 and 14% above. "All other categories" is 14%.
- **FBN outbound fee:** standard parcel 0.25–0.50 kg is **AED 9.50 when the price is above AED 25** (AED 7.50 at or below). That is the same figure as Amazon.ae's FBA fee for the same band (Aug 2025 card, see [[Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics]]).
- **Storage:** AED 1.75 per cubic foot per month. Stock held over 365 days costs AED 25 per cubic foot, and non-saleable stock held over 30 days costs AED 12 per cubic foot. Returns carry an admin fee of the lesser of AED 15 or 20% of the referral fee [1].
- **Licence:** the same UAE e-commerce licence and VAT set-up as Amazon.ae. See [[UAE E-commerce Seller Set-up: Licence, VAT and Corporate Tax]].

*Our view:* Noon costs no more per unit than Amazon.ae for this product profile. The real costs are a **second stock pool**, which ties up cash and raises the risk of stock-outs, plus listing and ad work.

### 2. Noon's UAE-to-KSA global store lane (cross-border, no Saudi entity)
Per Noon's seller guide (published 22 Apr 2026, modified 10 Nov 2026) [2][3]:
- **Enrolment is automatic and opt-out** for UAE sellers with a live UAE store selling via FBN (or an eligible FBP warehouse) and no existing KSA store. Noon creates the KSA store and links the UAE entity as **not VAT-registered in KSA**.
- **Before listings show,** an authorised signatory must sign the KSA terms, and each SKU needs a **12-digit HS code and a country of origin**. The seller is responsible for HS code accuracy.
- **VAT:** Noon is the principal that remits KSA VAT to ZATCA. **The seller needs no KSA VAT registration and files no KSA returns** [3]. This matches ZATCA's rule that a marketplace acting for a non-resident supplier is generally the deemed supplier [4][11].
- **Pricing:** Noon's pricing engine sets the KSA price. Its example: AED 100 including UAE VAT, less VAT gives AED 95, which converts to about SAR 97 before VAT, or about **SAR 112 to the customer** after 15% VAT [2].
- **Fees:** the same UAE FBN rate card. Noon says a "cross-border fee" replaces UAE VAT, and a VAT subsidy of about 10% keeps the **net payout equal to a domestic UAE sale**. However, KSA VAT of about 15% applies to Noon's fees, and an import VAT recovery fee of about 15% appears on statements [2]. *How these lines net out in cash is not verified: test it on one order.*
- **Duty:** on items priced **over SAR 1,000**, customs duty is added to the customer's price and does not reduce the seller's payout [2]. Below that, express parcels fall under a 0% tariff line (GCC Integrated Customs Tariff, from 1 Jan 2025 [13]), in line with ZATCA's SAR 1,000 duty exemption for e-store purchases [5].
- **Limits:** no replacements (refunds only), and Noon handles returns end to end. Items over 25 kg or 120 cm, and bulky items, are excluded, as are goods restricted in KSA. Products with a **warranty** need a KSA service arrangement, with repairs within 14 working days [2]. That matters for electronics, not for organisers or pet accessories.

*Our view:* this is the cheapest way into the largest GCC market. Its weaknesses: Saudi shoppers see a price about **9–10% higher** than the UAE price (15% VAT against 5%), delivery is cross-border, and returns are refund-only.

### 3. Amazon.sa
- **Account:** sellers with an existing unified account can add Saudi and UAE accounts from the Sell Globally dashboard. Amazon says a Middle East entity "may not" be needed [6]. Amazon.sa's own page says a **commercial registration is mandatory** but does not say whether it must be Saudi [7]. *Unverified for a UAE company: confirm in Seller Central (owner's existing proposed item "Amazon.ae Seller Central requirements check").*
- **Selling from Amazon.ae stock into KSA:** Amazon's official Remote Fulfillment terms cover **UK and EU lanes (the UAE only as a destination), not UAE to KSA** [8]. A T3 blog describes a UAE-to-KSA Remote Fulfillment feature with a per-sale cross-border fee, but cites no Amazon source [18]. *Treat it as unverified until it appears in Seller Central.*
- **Selling from your own stock in a Saudi FBA warehouse:**
  - **Importer:** someone in KSA must import the goods. The SABER portal is open only to importers, SASO-approved certification bodies and customs [10].
  - **SABER:** each product model needs a **Product Certificate of Conformity**, valid one year with a fee per model, and **every shipment needs a Shipment Certificate** [10]. Fees are not published on the page.
  - **KSA VAT:** goods in a Saudi warehouse when dispatch begins make the sale a **Saudi supply at 15%**. A non-resident seller selling to consumers must **register for KSA VAT whatever its turnover** (no SAR 375,000 threshold), including when a third party holds the stock [4]. Whether Amazon instead counts as the deemed supplier depends on the invoicing set-up [4]. *Adviser to confirm.*
  - **Duty:** under the GCC customs union, goods pay the common tariff (typically 5%) **once, at the first GCC entry point** (T3 [16]). Each state still inspects at its border, and the tariff varies by HS code. *Check the KSA rate on each shortlisted HS code.*
  - **Labelling:** from 1 Oct 2026, products under ten SASO technical regulations must show the Saudi supplier's name and CR number on the product (T3, citing a SASO circular [15]). None of the ten covers Global Trend Store's target niches, but strollers and child restraints are on the list.

*Our view:* Amazon.sa FBA makes sense only once KSA volume pays for an importer of record, SABER certificates and a Saudi VAT compliance cycle. The setup cost of those is not known (see Open questions).

### 4. Own store (Shopify or similar)
- **Payments:** the UAE is on Shopify's official list of countries for **Shopify Payments**; Saudi Arabia is not [9]. A T3 source puts the UAE rate at about **2.49% + AED 1** [17]. Another says access is limited to the Advanced plan; it is undated and gives no source. *Rates and plan limits are unverified.* Third-party gateways common in the GCC are Tap, Telr and Checkout.com (about 2–3%, T3 [17]). Shopify adds its own fee of **2% / 1% / 0.5%** (Basic / Shopify / Advanced) when a third-party gateway is used (T3 [17]; not checked against Shopify's pricing page, which was unreachable).
- **Selling to KSA from a UAE store:** if dispatch starts outside KSA, the seller charges no Saudi VAT. **15% VAT is collected at import "regardless of the value of the shipment"** [4][5], and duty applies above SAR 1,000 [5]. Saudi customs also charges a flat **SAR 15 declaration fee** on online-store parcels up to SAR 1,000 (from Oct 2024, [14]). Unless the seller ships with duties and taxes paid (DDP), the Saudi customer meets these charges at the door.
- **UAE VAT on those exports:** goods shipped to KSA are likely zero-rated as exports from the UAE. *Adviser to confirm, given the GCC VAT agreement amendments Saudi Arabia approved in June 2026 (Decision 887 [12]), which move intra-GCC goods toward destination-country VAT.*

## Worked example: AED 69 car back-seat organiser bundle, per unit (illustrative, Nov 2026)
Same assumptions as [[Amazon.ae and Noon Category Scan: Small, Light, Uncertified Niches]]: landed cost AED 18.35, standard parcel ≤0.5 kg, VAT-registered UAE seller, 12% automotive referral, ads at 15% of price.

| AED per unit | Amazon.ae FBA | Noon UAE FBN | Noon KSA lane | Shopify UAE (own 3PL) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenue (ex 5% VAT) | 65.71 | 65.71 | 65.71 (payout equal to UAE sale [2]) | 65.71 |
| Landed cost | 18.35 | 18.35 | 18.35 | 18.35 |
| Referral / payment fee | 8.28 | 8.28 [1] | 8.28 | 2.72 (2.49% + AED 1, unverified) |
| Fulfilment | 9.50 | 9.50 [1] | 9.50 | 18.00 (3PL pick-pack + courier, *judgement*) |
| Storage and returns allowance | 1.50 | 1.50 | 1.50 | 1.50 |
| Extra: KSA VAT on fees, if not recoverable | — | — | 2.67 (15% × 17.78, worst case) | — |
| **Contribution before marketing** | **28.08** | **28.08** | **25.41–28.08** | **25.14** |
| Marketing | 10.35 (ads 15%) | 10.35 | 10.35 | 20–30 (paid social to a cold audience, *judgement*) |
| **Net per unit** | **17.73** | **17.73** | **15.06–17.73** | **−4.86 to +5.14** |

Reading it:
- **Noon is a second Amazon on cost.** Every unit sold there is as profitable as one sold on Amazon.ae, provided it does not starve the Amazon listing of stock.
- **Except in pet accessories, where Noon is cheaper.** Noon charges 9% referral on most pet items (from 10 Sep 2026); Amazon.ae charges 15% once the price passes AED 50. An AED 59 pet bundle nets about AED 12.68 on Noon against AED 9.14 on Amazon.ae (verified 15 Nov 2026; see [[Amazon.ae and Noon Category Scan: Small, Light, Uncertified Niches]]).
- **The KSA lane loses at most about AED 2.70 a unit** in the worst case, and Noon says payout equals a UAE sale. That makes it the highest-return expansion: no fixed cost and no new entity.
- **A trend store on Shopify depends on acquisition cost.** Marketplaces bring buyers already searching. An own store has to buy every first visit. Judgement: without repeat purchases or a brand people search for, Shopify does not beat the marketplaces at AED 69.
- **DCS Golf reverses the maths.** On, say, an AED 1,800 bag, a 3% payment fee and AED 40 delivery are about 5% of revenue, against 15–25% in marketplace referral fees (Noon's "other bags" rate is 25% [1]). The brand story sells best on its own site.

## When does diversification beat doubling down?
Our view, as a decision rule:

| Add the channel when… | Because |
|---|---|
| Incremental contribution exceeds the new fixed costs plus the cost of carrying the extra stock | Otherwise it is busywork |
| The main listing has stable sell-through for about 60 days and the next reorder is due | You know the demand. Split the reorder, not the launch stock |
| The extra channel's buyers are not the same buyers at a lower margin | Noon and the KSA lane add shoppers; a Shopify store for the same SKU mostly competes with your own Amazon listing |
| Ad returns on Amazon are falling as spend rises | The next dirham works harder on a fresh channel |
| Monthly profit depends on one account | One suspension stops the income ([[Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics]]). A second live account is cheap insurance |

**Double down instead** when cash is the binding constraint (see the cash section of [[Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics]]): splitting a first order of 300–500 units across two warehouses makes stock-outs likely on both. **Ranking is fragile, and a stock-out on Amazon.ae costs more than a Noon sale earns.**

## Recommended sequence for Global Trend Store (judgement)
1. **Launch on Amazon.ae only**, as planned.
2. **First reorder: add Noon UAE FBN** with about 25–35% of the reorder. Same SKU, same pack, a 12-digit HS code and country of origin set from day one.
3. **Leave Noon's KSA lane on.** It enrols automatically once the Noon UAE store is live and costs nothing to keep. Check the first KSA payout statement against the table above.
4. **Amazon.sa FBA only when the KSA lane proves demand.** Judgement: a reasonable trigger is about 150+ KSA units a month for one SKU, plus written quotes for an importer of record, SABER certificates and KSA VAT compliance.
5. **No Shopify store for Global Trend Store.** Build it for **DCS Golf**, with Shopify Payments if the plan allows it, and ship GCC orders on DDP terms so Saudi buyers do not pay VAT and fees at the door.

## Key takeaways
- Noon UAE's FBN fees (Sep 2026) for a light parcel match Amazon.ae's: AED 9.50 fulfilment and 12% automotive referral.
- Noon's UAE-to-KSA lane is automatic and opt-out: no Saudi entity, no KSA VAT registration, no Saudi stock. Saudi shoppers pay about 9–10% more.
- Amazon.sa with your own FBA stock means a Saudi importer, SABER certificates per model and per shipment, and KSA VAT registration from the first sale.
- Shopify does not pay for a low-ticket trend store, but suits DCS Golf's high-ticket brand.
- Diversify at the first reorder, not at launch. Cash and stock-outs are the binding risks.

## What this means for the empire
- **Global Trend Store:** add a "Channels" step to the playbook: Amazon.ae at launch, Noon UAE plus the KSA lane at the first reorder, Amazon.sa as a gated option. Record a 12-digit HS code and country of origin for every SKU in the Commerce Lab now. Noon KSA needs them, and so do customs and SABER.
- **DCS Golf:** plan the online shop on Shopify (or similar) as the main channel. Marketplaces are for gifting-range accessories only. Bags are a "bags" category, where Noon charges 25% [1].
- **Structure:** one UAE e-commerce entity can run Amazon.ae, Noon UAE and Noon KSA. Do **not** create a Saudi entity or register for KSA VAT until the Amazon.sa gate in step 4 is met. Ask a tax adviser how the 2026 GCC VAT agreement changes affect UAE-to-KSA parcels before the DCS Golf store ships to Saudi Arabia.

## Open questions
- Does Amazon offer UAE-to-KSA Remote Fulfillment, and on what fees? Check in Seller Central (needs login).
- Can a UAE-only company open an Amazon.sa seller account, and who acts as importer of record into a Saudi Amazon warehouse? Ask Amazon Seller Support and 2 importer-of-record providers for quotes.
- SABER certificate costs per model, and the KSA tariff rate on the first shortlisted HS codes. Ask a SASO-approved certification body.
- What the Noon KSA lane statement shows in cash for one AED 69 order: the cross-border fee, the VAT subsidy, VAT on fees and the import VAT recovery fee.
- Shopify Payments UAE rates and plan eligibility, and UAE 3PL and GCC courier rates for DCS Golf parcels. Pull from Shopify admin and 2–3 courier quotes.
