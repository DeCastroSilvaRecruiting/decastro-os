# UAE and GCC Product Conformity and Import Rules for Amazon Sellers

> **Bottom line:** In the UAE, a product is either **regulated**, meaning it needs a MoIAT conformity certificate (ECAS), a GCC G-Mark, a TDRA type approval, a Dubai Municipality (Montaji) registration or a ministry permit before customs will clear it or before it may be sold, or it is **unregulated** and needs only correct labelling. Anything electrical, wireless, battery-powered, cosmetic or chemical, food-contact, for children, textile, health-related or edible is regulated. Wireless products add an extra hurdle: the trade licence itself must carry a telecom activity before TDRA will register you. Global Trend Store should launch only with non-electric, non-chemical, non-child, non-textile hard goods and treat each regulated category as a separate, costed project.

*Confidence is medium. The frameworks (ECAS, UAE RoHS, textiles, TDRA, Montaji, G-Mark, Dubai Customs restricted list, IATA battery rules) were read on official pages or in the legal texts. The full MoIAT list of regulated products is not published on an open page that could be fetched. The category list below therefore comes from notified-body summaries (SGS 2022–2024) and is incomplete. Amazon.ae's Seller Central help pages on restricted products and dangerous goods were **not reachable**, so the Amazon column is partial.*

## Who regulates what (as of Nov 2026)
- **MoIAT (Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology).** ESMA, the old standards authority, was merged into MoIAT, created in 2020 [17]. MoIAT runs **ECAS**, the Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme. ECAS issues a UAE Certificate of Conformity for products under UAE technical regulations. It requires a valid trade licence and a test report from an accredited lab [1]. Notified bodies report that ECAS certificates last **1 year**, and the alternative **EQM** (Emirates Quality Mark) lasts **3 years** [19][21]. MoIAT also attests GSO certificates for products under GCC regulations, at **AED 50 per copy** (undated page, [2]).
- **Marks changed recently.** Ministerial Resolution No. 137 of 2024 replaced the ECAS logo and conformity marks [22]. MoIAT gave a **6-month** transition for imports and **12 months** for stock already in the market (Apr 2025, [3]). A French trade-agency note says ECAS moved to **full application in 2026**, with customs checking certificates through MoIAT's Conformity Hub portal (Feb 2026, [23]; Tier 2, not confirmed on a MoIAT page).
- **GSO G-Mark.** GSO is the GCC Standardization Organization. Its G-Mark is compulsory for products under GCC technical regulations, with toys and low-voltage equipment the first priorities [11]. Each product carries a **GCTS** (GSO Conformity Tracking Symbol) whose QR code customs can scan [12][13]. GSO lists the UAE among the states that apply it [12][13].
- **TDRA (Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority).** Approves radio and telecom equipment, and registers the dealers that import or sell it [6][7].
- **Dubai Municipality (Montaji platform).** Registers consumer products for the Dubai market before import [8].
- **Other bodies.** MoCCAE (Ministry of Climate Change and Environment) controls live animals, plants, fertilisers and insecticides. MOHAP (Ministry of Health and Prevention) controls pharmaceuticals and medical instruments [9]. Medical-device services have moved to the **Emirates Drug Establishment (EDE)**, the transfer reported complete by late 2025 ([26], Tier 3; confirm with EDE).

## Labelling
- **Language.** Labels in Arabic, or Arabic and English. Arabic stickers are allowed but should be applied **before export, not on arrival**. Country of origin is a required label item (ITA, Aug 2025, [18]).
- **Textiles** [4]. Show fibre composition by weight percentage, plus the country of manufacture, manufacturer, **importer** and trademark. Add the ECAS mark, care and storage information, and a batch code.
- **Toys** [12]. Safety instructions in Arabic. The manufacturer's name or trademark and address on the toy. A type, batch or serial number. Age warnings. A scannable GCTS on the toy or its outer packaging.
- **Wireless** [7]. A TDRA conformity card with QR code on the box, and shown under the product **on websites** too. That means the Amazon listing image.
- **Regulated products** must carry the post-2024 ECAS marks, and energy labels where the product requires one [3][22].
- **Dubai Municipality products.** Labels must be clear, indelible and hard to remove [8].
- **Amazon.** An FNSKU on every unit (see [[China Sourcing Playbook for UAE Private-Label Importers]]).

*Judgement for every product:* Arabic and English name, origin ("Made in China"), importer name and address (your UAE company), batch code and any warnings, printed at the factory. It costs nothing at artwork stage and closes most labelling risk.

## Batteries and dangerous goods
**By air** (IATA DGR 67th edition, guidance dated 1 Jan 2026, [14]):
- Batteries shipped alone (**UN 3480**) and **power banks** are banned on passenger aircraft and must fly on cargo aircraft only, at **≤30% state of charge**.
- From **1 Jan 2026**, batteries *packed with* equipment (**UN 3481**, PI 966) must also be at ≤30% charge.
- For batteries *contained in* equipment (PI 967), ≤30% is strongly recommended.
- The Section II small-battery limit is **≤100 Wh per lithium-ion battery** (≤20 Wh per cell), with the lithium battery mark.
- Every cell and battery needs a **UN 38.3 test summary**, available on request.

**By sea.** Small lithium batteries can move under **IMDG Special Provision 188** without a dangerous goods declaration, but the packages must still be marked. Insurers and some states are pushing the IMO to cap this exemption per container (Sep 2026, [27]). The Wh limits were not read from the IMDG Code itself.

**Amazon FBA.** Listing an item with a lithium battery, aerosol, perfume, nail polish, sanitiser, strong glue or similar triggers Amazon's **dangerous goods review**. You may need to upload a Safety Data Sheet or exemption sheet, and some items need the FBA dangerous-goods programme [16] (US page; Amazon.ae rules not reachable). *Judgement:* each battery SKU adds weeks and a failure point. Avoid them in year one.

## Prohibited and restricted
Dubai Customs **prohibits** the following (updated 10 Nov 2026, [9]):
- narcotics;
- gambling devices;
- nylon fishing nets;
- live pigs;
- **used, reconditioned or retreaded tyres**;
- radioactive goods;
- paan and betel leaves;
- items contrary to Islamic faith or public morals.

**Restricted** goods need the controlling authority's approval [9][10]:

| Goods | Approving authority |
|---|---|
| Animals, plants, fertilisers and insecticides | MoCCAE |
| Weapons and fireworks | MoD / MoI |
| Pharmaceuticals and medical instruments | MOHAP, now EDE for devices |
| Media | National Media Council |
| New tyres, e-cigarettes and hookah | ESMA, now MoIAT |
| **Transmitters and radio equipment** | TDRA |
| Alcohol | Dubai Police |
| Food, personal care and cosmetics | Dubai Municipality |
| Rough diamonds | Kimberley Process Office |

TDRA separately bans GSM jammers, boosters and scanning receivers [6]. *Judgement:* also avoid knives and weapon-like items, laser pointers, spy cameras or drones, and anything with religious imagery or that could be read as immodest.

## Safe-by-design short list (judgement)
These types **appear** to need no UAE pre-market certificate, registration or permit. The judgement rests on the absence of any scheme in the sources above, **not on a confirmed MoIAT negative**. MoIAT keeps adding regulations.

- Non-electric home organisation and storage: hooks, racks, drawer dividers, cable tidies.
- Desk and office accessories: stands, organisers, non-electronic phone or tablet holders.
- Phone and tablet cases, and screen protectors (no electronics).
- Leather and PU small goods: wallets, cardholders, key fobs, straps. Leather is excluded from the textile regulation [4].
- Non-powered hand and garden tools.
- Fitness accessories without electronics: resistance bands, grips, mats. Check that mats are not textile-dominant.
- Pet accessories that are not food or chemical: leads, bowls. Bowls may count as food contact; confirm.
- Golf accessories without electronics: ball markers, divot tools, tees, leather headcovers.

**Borderline, confirm first:**
- Anything touching food or drink (food-contact rules).
- Anything ≥80% textile: towels, canvas items, cushions.
- Anything a child might use.
- Car accessories that could count as spare parts.

## Key takeaways
- Regulated means a certificate or registration **before import**. Customs, not Amazon, is the first gate.
- Wireless products are the hardest: per-model TDRA approval, dealer registration, a telecom activity on the licence and a permit per shipment.
- Dubai Municipality fines **AED 1,000 per unregistered product** (May 2025, [8]) for the Montaji categories.
- From 1 Jan 2026, batteries packed with equipment must ship by air at ≤30% charge. Batteries mean Amazon hazmat review.
- Build the first range from the safe-by-design list. Add regulated lines only once a winner justifies the certification cost.

## What this means for the empire
- **Global Trend Store.** Filter "trending" products through this table **before** sampling. Most viral China products are gadgets: Bluetooth, USB, rechargeable or LED. They fall in the hardest category: ECAS or RoHS, plus TDRA if wireless, plus dangerous goods.
  - **Worked example:** a 1,000-unit Bluetooth gadget. On top of the landed cost in [[Amazon FBA from China: Model, Rules and Economics]], it needs a TDRA dealer registration and a telecom licence activity, a type approval per model, UAE RoHS/ECAS (AED 4,300 per application covering up to 45 products, a **2018** fee from [24]; re-quote it), and an Amazon dangerous-goods review. *Judgement:* several thousand dirhams and 1–3 months before the first sale. That cost is wasted on the 70% of launches that fail. A silicone desk organiser needs none of it.
  - Ask the licensing agent in [[UAE E-commerce Seller Set-up: Licence, VAT and Corporate Tax]] to add a telecom activity only if wireless becomes a deliberate line.
- **DCS Golf.**
  - Leather bags, headcovers and leather accessories appear **unregulated** (leather is excluded from the textile regulation, [4]).
  - **Canvas bags** could fall under textile regulation 54/2019 if ≥80% textile by weight. The outdoor-use exclusion may cover golf bags, but that is untested: ask MoIAT.
  - Bag **towels** are textile.
  - **Rangefinders** = ECAS laser plus TDRA if Bluetooth.
  - **GPS watches or tags** = TDRA (the regulation covers reception too, [7]) plus a battery.
  - **Heated gloves or covers** = RoHS plus battery dangerous goods.
  - Making in the UAE does not escape these rules: they apply to products "manufactured" as well as imported [4][5].
  - Use **ostrich leather** from the Al Ain farm only after confirming CITES status with MoCCAE. Farmed ostrich is generally understood to be outside CITES; not verified here.
- **What a licensed customs broker or MoIAT should confirm:**
  - The HS code of each SKU, and whether Dubai Customs flags it for an ECAS, TDRA or DM certificate.
  - Whether MoIAT requires ECAS for toys **in addition to** G-Mark.
  - Whether power banks and small USB devices need ECAS.
  - Whether the textile rule applies to canvas golf bags.

## Open questions
- MoIAT's current full list of regulated products (2026) and the ECAS fee schedule. Ask MoIAT's call centre or a notified body (SGS, TÜV, Intertek) for a written scope check per HS code.
- The Amazon.ae documents required per restricted category, and whether Amazon.ae demands an ECAS or TDRA number at listing. Read them in Seller Central once the account is live.
- Dubai Municipality's e-commerce guideline for consumer products (DM-HSD-GU18) could not be opened; it may impose seller duties for online listings.
- Whether the Digital Commerce Law (Federal Decree-Law 14/2023, with penalties under Cabinet Resolution 200/2025 in force from 28 Nov 2025, [25]) adds product-information duties for sellers.
